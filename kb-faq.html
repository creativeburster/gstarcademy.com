How do I resolve floating point exception errors in Fluent solvers?
Check mesh quality metrics (skewness < 0.9, aspect ratio < 100), reduce under-relaxation factors (URF) for pressure and momentum, verify boundary condition physical bounds, and start with first-order discretisation schemes before switching to high-fidelity second-order.
What is the recommended practice for capturing CFD boundary layers in Fluent?
Generate inflation layers at the solid boundaries. Calculate the first cell height using the desired y+ target (typically y+ < 1 for resolving, or y+ = 30-100 for wall functions) based on the target Reynolds number.

Source · Ansys Mechanical Technical Reference Frequently Asked Questions

How do I fix unconverged nonlinear structural simulations in ANSYS?
Review the solver output for force convergence criteria, enable automatic time stepping, identify separating regions with contact diagnostics, increase contact pinball radius, and apply small stabilization damping factors if rigid-body motion occurs.
What is the difference between bonded and no-separation contacts?
Bonded contacts prevent all sliding and separation (faces act as glued). No-separation contacts allow sliding along the face tangent but prevent separation along the normal, representing a frictionless guide slider.

Source · Ansys Fluent Documentation Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between pressure-based and density-based solvers in Ansys Fluent?
The pressure-based solver (now coupled by default) targets incompressible and mildly compressible flows and converges robustly for most industrial cases; the density-based solver targets strongly compressible, high-Mach flows with shocks. For subsonic external aero or internal flows, start pressure-based coupled.
What do y+ wall treatment requirements mean for my mesh in Ansys Fluent?
With enhanced wall treatments, keep the first-cell y+ around 1 to resolve the viscous sublayer; with wall functions, target y+ between 30 and 300 and avoid values near 1 or in the buffer layer. Build boundary-layer inflation so at least 10-15 cells resolve the boundary layer, and keep growth ratios under 1.2.
When is a steady-state RANS solution acceptable instead of a transient (URANS/LES) simulation in Fluent?
Steady RANS is acceptable when time-averaged quantities are the goal and unsteadiness is weak or statistical; unsteady methods are required for vortex shedding, large separation, acoustic sources, or when residuals and monitors will not converge. Judge by monitoring integrated quantities for persistent oscillation rather than residuals alone.

Source · Ansys Mechanical Documentation Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between force-controlled and displacement-controlled loading in Ansys Mechanical, and why does it matter for post-buckling?
Force (load) control loses stability at limit points, so an eigenvalue or geometrically nonlinear buckling analysis under load control terminates at the critical load. Displacement or arc-length control can continue past the limit point and trace the post-buckling path, which is why nonlinear buckling studies displace a remote point instead of applying a force.
How do contact detection methods (node-to-surface vs surface-to-surface) affect results in Ansys Mechanical?
Node-to-surface detects penetration at nodes and handles large relative sliding with lower cost but can leave contact pressure patchy; surface-to-surface (the default) integrates over projected areas for smoother pressure distribution and better accuracy for bonded/frictional faces. For bolted or gasketed faces, prefer surface-to-surface with relevant correction.
What does 'weak springs' do in an Ansys Mechanical static structural analysis, and what is the safer alternative?
Weak springs add very soft ground springs to stabilize rigid-body motion of an insufficiently constrained model. They can mask modeling errors and add phantom stiffness, so the safer practice is to verify that the model is properly constrained (or use inertia relief) and only use weak springs as a diagnostic, never in the reported load path.

Source · Bentley Openroads Documentation Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between civil fixtures and templates in OpenRoads Designer?
Templates define the cross-section assembly — points, components, and their parametric constraints that build the corridor; civil fixtures (like End Condition Objects) place standardized features such as guardrails or ditch shapes. How do parametric constraints differ from point controls in OpenRoads corridors?
Parametric constraints vary template parameters (lane width, slope) along the corridor by station ranges; point controls move or lock individual template points to horizontal/vertical elements or 3D geometry. What is the purpose of terrain models in OpenRoads Designer?
Terrain models aggregate survey points, breaklines, and filters into a smart surface that supports contours, drainage queries, and volume calculations. Because terrains are feature-based rather than triangulated black boxes, edits to source data regenerate the surface, and multiple terrains (existing, proposed) support cut/fill analysis.

How do I resolve singular matrix solver errors in COMSOL?
Check that all boundaries have sufficient boundary conditions (prevent rigid body motion), verify that material properties are defined for all domains, scale coupled variables so their magnitudes are close, and use a direct solver (MUMPS) instead of iterative solvers.
What is the difference between Fully Coupled and Segregated solvers?
Fully Coupled solvers assemble all physical equations into a single giant matrix and solve it simultaneously, which is accurate but memory-intensive. Segregated solvers solve each physics sequentially, updating shared variables iteratively, saving substantial memory.

How do I import a STEP file into SOLIDWORKS?
File > Open > select the .STEP/.STP file. SOLIDWORKS imports as a dumb solid (no feature tree). Use FeatureWorks (Premium) to attempt feature recognition, or work with the imported body directly using Direct Edit tools (Move Face, Delete Face, Replace Face).
Why does my assembly rebuild so slowly?
Most common causes: too many redundant mates, large in-context external references, components in resolved mode that could be lightweight or large-assembly mode, and high-detail decorative components left in the assembly. Use Performance Evaluation (formerly AssemblyXpert) for a diagnostic.
Can SOLIDWORKS open Inventor files?
Not directly. Use STEP or Parasolid export from Inventor and open as static geometry in SOLIDWORKS. The third-party CADfix tool can do guided feature translation if feature parity is critical.

Source · Staad.Pro Documentation Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between STAAD.Pro's STAAD space and STAAD plane model types?
STAAD space defines a 3D structure with all six degrees of freedom at nodes (frames in any direction); STAAD plane restricts a 2D frame in one plane. Use space for real 3D frames, plane only for idealized 2D portal or truss checks where out-of-plane actions are intentionally excluded.
How do STAAD.Pro load combinations differ from repeat load cases?
Load combinations algebraically sum ANALYSIS results (valid for linear elastic cases), while REPEAT LOAD cases create a new load case by referencing prior case loads and re-run analysis effects — required when nonlinearity (tension-only members, supports, cables) makes superposition invalid. Choose repeat loads whenever any nonlinearity is present.
What does STAAD.Pro's DIRECT vs STAAD design method mean in steel design parameters?
In AISC 360 parameters, DIRECT invokes the Direct Analysis Method (reduced stiffness, notional loads, K=1) for stability-sensitive frames; the alternative ASD/LRFD interaction methods rely on calculated K factors from alignment charts. Direct analysis is the modern default when capturing second-order effects honestly.

What is the difference between NX and UG?
Same product, different name. UG (UniGraphics) is the legacy name from 1973 until 2002, when Siemens (then EDS) rebranded to NX. Veteran users still say 'UG.' The .prt file format is continuous across the rename — UG files can open in modern NX.
What is Synchronous Technology and why does NX have it?
ST is direct-face editing on a body without disturbing parametric history. NX has it because it solves two pain points: editing imported geometry without history, and editing native parts when parametric edits would be tedious. Mixing ST with parametric is uniquely NX's capability.
Does NX require Teamcenter?
Not technically — NX can run with the native filesystem. But production deployments almost always use Teamcenter (or NX X with Teamcenter X for cloud). Without it, large-assembly performance and revision control become unmanageable.
Can NX run on macOS or Linux?
Linux: yes, certain Red Hat Enterprise Linux and SUSE distributions are supported for on-premise NX. macOS: native no, but NX X (cloud variant) can be used from any platform via browser.
How does NX CAM compare to Mastercam or Fusion 360 CAM?
NX CAM is widely considered top-tier for 5-axis, mold/die, and complex multi-axis machining. Mastercam is competitive especially for 2D/2.5D and shop-floor adoption; Fusion 360 CAM is excellent for hobbyist through small-shop work but less mature for production 5-axis. Both Siemens products. NX is high-end, used by automotive OEMs and tier-1s, with Teamcenter PLM. Solid Edge is mid-market, similar tier to SOLIDWORKS, mainstream machinery and consumer products. Both share Synchronous Technology heritage.

Source · Siemens Solid Edge Documentation Frequently Asked Questions

What is Siemens Synchronous Technology in Solid Edge?
Synchronous Technology combines direct (history-free) editing with parametric design intent: you can push-pull faces on imported STEP geometry while recognized relationships (concentric, symmetric, tangent) keep design rules. What is the difference between Solid Edge's Ordered and Synchronous sheet metal environments?
Ordered sheet metal builds a feature history (flange, contour flange, dimple) that regenerates top-down; Synchronous sheet metal edits faces directly with machine-recognized bends remaining associative. Both produce flat patterns with the same bend tables — choose Ordered for design-intent-heavy parts and Synchronous for imported or concept work.
How does Solid Edge manage standard parts in assemblies?
Solid Edge ships a Standard Parts database (bolts, bearings) with family-of-parts configurations that display simplified/spoolable representations for large assemblies.
