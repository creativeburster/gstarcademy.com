Domains & industries

Where CAD work lands in the real economy

“Domain” here means professional context—not a curriculum chapter. The same CAD literacy can serve AEC, mechanical manufacturing, product/industrial design, or a deliberately cross-cutting role that manages formats and collaboration. Pick a card to jump down; each lane links outward to tutorials, tracks, terms, or maps so this page stays a portal—not a PDF dump.

AEC AEC · built environment

BIM, coordination, IFC, detailing for buildings & infrastructure. Outputs: plans, models, fabrication packages.

Read lane → MFG MFG · mechanical parts

Modeling for fabrication: tolerances, drawings, BOM, STEP/DXF handoff to CAM or suppliers.

Read lane → PD Product · design intent

Form-first iteration: surfacing, ergonomics, prototyping (3D print/SLA), visuals for decision makers.

Read lane → X Cross · formats & teams

Interop, roles, and “who owns the truth” when several tools share one project (DWG ↔ RVT ↔ STEP).

AEC lane · what to learn after basics

Structures, MEP, civils

Start with disciplined documentation: sheet sets, shared coordinates, and reusable detail libraries. Then move into discipline-specific authoring (structural vs MEP vs architecture) and coordination via BIM workflows. OpenBIM handoff often means IFC plus model checks before site work.

  • Tutorials tagged AEC / BIM / Revit-class tools
  • Term cards: BIM, IFC, Xref, …
  • See how AEC branches in the literacy graph

MFG lane · from part to toolpath

Machining, sheet metal, assemblies

Mechanical CAD emphasizes tolerances, datums, and reproducible drawings. Assemblies drive BOMs and revision control; solid models feed STEP/Parasolid exchange and CAM for CNC or additive paths. Stress/thermal lives in CAE—which this lane touches but does not replace.

  • Mechanical CAD & drawing tutorials
  • Terms hub — CAD vs CAE vs CAM in context

Product / industrial lane · concept to prototype

Aesthetic + manufacturable

Industrial designers iterate form, material, and user fit before engineering locks dimensions. Outputs include renders, SLA patterns, ergonomic studies, and design variants that downstream teams translate into tooling.

  • Surface & product-design oriented finds
  • Where popular surfacing/industrial stacks sit

Cross-platform literacy · integration & custody of data

Multi-vendor pipelines

Real projects rarely stick to one app. Literacy means knowing how DWG, STEP, IFC, neutral formats, and PDM workflows interact—and who resolves clashes when numbering, units, or model fidelity diverges. Useful when you orchestrate subcontractors or translate between authoring teams.
