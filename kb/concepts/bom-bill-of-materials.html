Bill of Materials (BOM)

BOM generation and management in CAD: engineering BOM vs manufacturing BOM, indented lists, and ERP integration.

By Gstarcademy Editorial Team Updated 2026-07-28 Editorial process

Definition & Context

A Bill of Materials (BOM) is the structured list of parts, quantities, and references needed to build a product or assembly. CAD generates BOMs from the assembly structure; types include the EBOM (engineering, as-designed), MBOM (manufacturing, as-built with process/consumables), and service BOM.

BOMs carry item numbers, descriptions, quantities, and often material and source; they link CAD to PDM/PLM and ERP, and drive purchasing, planning, and costing.

Why It Matters

The BOM is the backbone connecting design to procurement and manufacturing. Errors — wrong quantities, missing items, EBOM/MBOM mismatch — ripple into purchasing and production delays.

The key distinction is EBOM vs MBOM: the EBOM reflects how the product is designed (functional structure from the CAD assembly), while the MBOM reflects how it is built (assembly sequence, consumables like adhesive and fasteners, phantom/bulk items, and plant-specific substitutions). Keeping them linked but separate lets engineering and manufacturing each own their view without one corrupting the other — which is exactly the reconciliation PDM/PLM systems automate rather than leaving to spreadsheets.

Best Practices

  • Generate BOMs from the model structure, not manual lists, to stay in sync.
  • Maintain consistent item numbering and part metadata.
  • Reconcile EBOM and MBOM for manufacturing needs (consumables, process parts).
  • Integrate BOM with PDM/PLM and ERP to avoid re-keying.

Common Pitfalls

  • Manually maintained BOMs that drift from the model.
  • EBOM used directly in manufacturing without an MBOM.
  • Inconsistent part numbers/descriptions causing duplicates.
  • Quantity errors from configuration/suppressed components.
