Elevator & Lift Design

Professional guide to elevator & lift design — tools, workflows, software recommendations, and best practices for engineers and designers.

By Gstarcademy Editorial TeamUpdated 2026-07-28

Definition & Context

Elevator/lift design engineers vertical-transportation systems — car, counterweight, guide rails, ropes/belts or hydraulics, drives, and safety devices (governor, brakes, buffers). Design covers hoistway/machine-room layout, load and speed, traffic analysis, and code compliance (ASME A17.1 / EN 81).

Layout is modelled in CAD/BIM and coordinated with the building shaft; traffic calculations size the number, speed, and capacity of cars for the building's population.

Why It Matters

Elevators are safety-critical, code-heavy systems and a key factor in building usability. Correct traffic sizing and code-compliant safety design ensure efficient, safe vertical transport.

Tools & Software

  • AutoCAD/Revit (layout)
  • elevator traffic analysis tools
  • manufacturer design software

Best Practices

  • Perform traffic analysis to size car count, speed, and capacity.
  • Coordinate hoistway/pit/overhead and machine space with the building.
  • Design safety systems and comply with ASME A17.1 / EN 81.
  • Plan for accessibility and firefighter/evacuation operation.

Common Pitfalls

  • Undersized traffic capacity causing long waits.
  • Hoistway/pit/overhead clashes with the structure.
  • Non-compliance with lift safety codes.
  • Ignoring accessibility and emergency operation requirements.

Standards & Interoperability

  • ASME A17.1 / EN 81
