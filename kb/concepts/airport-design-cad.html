Airport Design in CAD

Professional guide to airport design in cad — tools, workflows, software recommendations, and best practices for engineers and designers.

By GstarCAD Academy Engineering Editorial Board (Suzhou Gstarsoft Co., Ltd.) Reviewed by GstarCAD Academy Editorial Team Last reviewed 2026-08-16 Editorial Process & Testbed

Definition & Context

Airport design encompasses airside (runways, taxiways, aprons) and landside (terminals, roads, parking) civil engineering. Airside geometry follows strict ICAO/FAA standards for runway/taxiway dimensions, separations, grades, pavement design, and obstacle-limitation surfaces (OLS).

Civil tools model pavements, grading, and drainage while BIM covers terminals; obstacle surfaces and airspace analysis ensure safe approaches, and pavement design follows FAARFIELD/PCN methods.

Why It Matters

Airports are safety-critical infrastructure with exacting geometric and clearance standards. Errors in separations, grades, or obstacle surfaces have direct aviation-safety and regulatory consequences.

Tools & Software

  • Autodesk Civil 3D / InfraWorks
  • Bentley OpenSite/OpenRoads
  • FAARFIELD (pavement)

Best Practices

  • Design airside geometry to ICAO Annex 14 / FAA AC standards.
  • Verify obstacle-limitation surfaces and approach clearances.
  • Design pavements (rigid/flexible) with correct traffic/aircraft loads.
  • Coordinate drainage, grading, and airside/landside interfaces.

Common Pitfalls

  • Runway/taxiway separations or grades violating ICAO/FAA limits.
  • Obstacles penetrating limitation surfaces unchecked.
  • Under-designed pavement for the aircraft mix.
  • Poor airside drainage causing ponding/aquaplaning.

Standards & Interoperability

  • ICAO Annex 14
  • FAA AC 150/5300 series
  • Eurocode 7 (Geotechnical Design - EN 1997)
  • ISO 19650 (BIM for Civil Infrastructure)
