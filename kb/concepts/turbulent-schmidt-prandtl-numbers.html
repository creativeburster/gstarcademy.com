Turbulent Prandtl (Prt) & Schmidt (Sct) Numbers in Heat & Species Transport

Engineering significance of Turbulent Prandtl and Schmidt numbers in CFD. Calibrate turbulent thermal diffusivity and species dispersion in boundary layers.

By Gstarcademy Technical Review Board Updated 2026-10-16 Editorial process

Strict adherence to international ISO, ASME, and buildingSMART technical standards.
🛡️ Peer-Reviewed Methodology:

1. Definition & Mathematical Engineering Foundations

In turbulent fluid dynamics, heat and chemical species are transported not only by molecular diffusion, but primarily by turbulent eddy mixing. Under Reynolds-Averaged Navier-Stokes (RANS) formulations, the Reynolds analogy relates turbulent momentum diffusivity (eddy viscosity $\nu_t$) to turbulent thermal diffusivity ($\alpha_t$) and turbulent mass diffusivity ($D_t$) via non-dimensional ratios: the Turbulent Prandtl Number ($Pr_t = \nu_t / \alpha_t$) and the Turbulent Schmidt Number ($Sc_t = \nu_t / D_t$). Properly calibrating these values is critical for accurate conjugate heat transfer, combustion, and pollutant dispersion simulations.

2. Technical Architecture & Key Formulations

  • Standard Default Constant ($Pr_t = 0.85$ to $0.9$): In standard air boundary layers, setting $Pr_t = 0.85$ (boundary layers) or $0.7$ (free shear layers) provides solid first-order agreement with experimental thermal profiles.
  • Liquid Metal Thermal Disruption ($Pr_t \gg 1$): For low-Prandtl liquid metals (liquid sodium or lead-bismuth in nuclear reactors, where molecular $Pr \approx 0.005–0.02$), the Reynolds analogy breaks down completely. $Pr_t$ must be adjusted to values between 1.5 and 3.0 using Kays or Jischa correlations.
  • Turbulent Schmidt Number for Gas Dispersion ($Sc_t \approx 0.7$): Governs how methane gas leaks or smoke plumes diffuse in windy outdoor plant environments; miscalibrated $Sc_t$ distorts explosion hazard zone boundaries.
  • Variable Prt Formulation in Compressible Flow: In supersonic and hypersonic boundary layers ($Ma > 3$), aerodynamic friction heating drives significant cross-stream variations in $Pr_t$, requiring dynamic algebraic thermal models.

3. Industry Failure Modes & Risk Mitigation

A critical engineering failure in liquid-metal cooled electronics or nuclear heat exchangers occurs when CFD engineers leave the default $Pr_t = 0.85$ active in the solver. This overpredicts turbulent heat transfer by 200%, dangerously underestimating component peak temperatures.

4. Production Governance & Metrological Standards

Modern multi-physics CFD solvers allow regional or field-dependent specification of $Pr_t$ and $Sc_t$, ensuring rigorous validation against empirical heat transfer correlations (such as Dittus-Boelter and Lyon-Martinelli).
