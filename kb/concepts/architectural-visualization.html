Architectural Visualization Workflow

End-to-end archviz workflow: model preparation, material assignment, lighting, rendering, and post-production.

By Gstarcademy Editorial Team Updated 2026-06-30 Editorial process

Definition & Context

Architectural visualization (arch-viz) produces images, animations, and interactive experiences of buildings before they are built. It combines BIM/CAD geometry, PBR materials, realistic lighting (sun studies, HDRI, interior lighting), entourage (people, vegetation, cars), and post-production.

Pipelines range from offline photoreal (3ds Max + V-Ray/Corona) to real-time (Enscape, Twinmotion, Unreal) for walkthroughs and VR. Sun/shadow studies and accurate context are key differentiators for AEC clients.

Why It Matters

Arch-viz sells designs and wins competitions — it lets clients and planners understand space, light, and materials, and validates design decisions long before construction.

Best Practices

  • Base scenes on accurate model geometry and real-world sun/location data.
  • Invest in lighting and composition; entourage adds life and scale.
  • Use real-time engines for iterative client review, offline for hero shots.
  • Manage assets/textures for performance in large scenes.

Common Pitfalls

  • Empty, lifeless scenes with no entourage or context.
  • Incorrect sun position/date undermining daylight credibility.
  • Over-detailed geometry crippling real-time performance.
  • Inconsistent materials/lighting between shots.
