Tensile & Membrane Structure Design

Professional guide to tensile & membrane structure design — tools, workflows, software recommendations, and best practices for engineers and designers.

By Gstarcademy Editorial TeamUpdated 2026-06-30

Definition & Context

Tensile/membrane structure design creates lightweight roofs and canopies from prestressed fabric (PTFE/PVC) or cable nets, whose shape derives from form-finding — the equilibrium of prestress and boundary conditions. Analysis covers form-finding, load analysis (wind, snow), and cutting-pattern generation.

Specialist tools (Ansys/Forten, EASY, Rhino+Kangaroo/kiwi!3d) find the minimal-surface form, analyse membrane stresses, and unroll double-curved surfaces into flat cutting patterns with compensation.

Why It Matters

Membrane structures are shape-dependent: the geometry IS the structure. Correct form-finding and cutting-pattern generation are essential — there is no arbitrary shape choice as in rigid structures.

Tools & Software

  • Rhino + Kangaroo / kiwi!3d
  • EASY (technet)
  • Forten / Ansys

Best Practices

  • Form-find the surface for a valid prestress equilibrium.
  • Analyse under wind/snow including large-displacement effects.
  • Generate compensated cutting patterns for the fabric.
  • Design boundary cables, masts, and anchorages for the reactions.

Common Pitfalls

  • Imposing an arbitrary shape that isn't in equilibrium.
  • Linear analysis ignoring large displacements of membranes.
  • Cutting patterns without compensation, causing wrinkles/slack.
  • Under-designing masts/anchorages for high membrane reactions.

Standards & Interoperability

  • Eurocode wind/snow
