Generative Shape Design

CATIA's Generative Shape Design workbench for creating complex freeform surfaces with curvature continuity.

By Gstarcademy Editorial Team Updated 2026-07-28 Editorial process

Definition & Context

Generative Shape Design (GSD) is CATIA's core surfacing workbench: it builds wireframe and freeform surfaces (extrude, sweep, multi-section/loft, fill, blend) with control over curvature continuity (point/tangent/curvature) for high-quality form. Surfaces are then joined, trimmed, and thickened or closed into solids.

GSD is fully associative and parametric within CATIA's history tree and feeds hybrid design (surfaces + solids) used in automotive, aerospace, and consumer-product form.

Why It Matters

GSD is how CATIA users create complex, high-quality freeform geometry that prismatic solid features can't — the basis of styled exterior surfaces and blended transitions.

Curvature-continuity control is what separates a fair, cleanly-reflecting Class-A-quality surface from a visibly kinked one.

Best Practices

  • Build with the right continuity (tangent vs curvature) for the surface quality needed
  • Keep clean, associative wireframe references so surfaces update parametrically
  • Analyze surfaces (curvature/zebra) to catch discontinuities early
  • Structure the tree so surface geometry is maintainable and reusable

Common Pitfalls

  • Insufficient continuity leaving visible kinks or reflection breaks
  • Messy underlying wireframe breaking associative updates
  • Gaps/overlaps at joins preventing a clean closed solid
