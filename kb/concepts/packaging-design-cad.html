Packaging Design in 3D

Professional guide to packaging design in 3d — tools, workflows, software recommendations, and best practices for engineers and designers.

By Gstarcademy Editorial TeamUpdated 2026-06-30

Definition & Context

Packaging/structural-design CAD develops cartons, corrugated boxes, blisters, and containers — dielines (flat patterns), 3-D folding, and material/strength engineering. Specialist tools (Esko ArtiosCAD, Adobe/Studio) generate standard (FEFCO) and custom dielines and validate folding and stacking strength.

Design balances protection, material efficiency, machinability (die-cutting, gluing), palletisation, and branding, producing dielines that go straight to die-making.

Why It Matters

Packaging must protect the product, minimise material/cost, run on machines, and sell on shelf. Accurate dielines and strength engineering prevent failures and waste at scale.

Tools & Software

  • Esko ArtiosCAD
  • Adobe Illustrator (dielines)
  • box compression tools

Best Practices

  • Use standard (FEFCO) styles where possible; validate custom folds in 3-D.
  • Engineer stacking/compression strength for the supply chain.
  • Optimise material use and nesting for cost.
  • Design for die-cutting, gluing, and filling-line machinability.

Common Pitfalls

  • Dielines that don't fold/glue correctly in production.
  • Under-strength packs failing stacking/transit.
  • Wasteful layouts inflating material cost.
  • Designs incompatible with filling/packing machinery.

Standards & Interoperability

  • FEFCO codes
  • ISTA transit testing
