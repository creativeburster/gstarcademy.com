Digital Twin in Engineering

Digital twin technology: connecting CAD/BIM models to real-world sensor data for monitoring, simulation, and prediction.

By Gstarcademy Editorial Team Updated 2026-06-30 Editorial process

Definition & Context

A digital twin is a living virtual representation of a physical asset kept in sync with real-time data from IoT sensors, BMS, and operational systems. Unlike a static as-built BIM, a twin is continuously updated and used for monitoring, simulation, prediction, and control across the asset's operational life.

Twins span a maturity spectrum: descriptive (current state), informative (integrated data), predictive (forecasting via analytics/ML), and autonomous (closed-loop control). Platforms include Azure Digital Twins, Bentley iTwin, and Siemens/NVIDIA Omniverse-based solutions.

Why It Matters

Digital twins unlock value in the operational phase — 80%+ of an asset's life-cycle cost — through predictive maintenance, energy optimisation, and scenario simulation grounded in real data.

Best Practices

  • Start from a reliable as-built/handover model with clean asset data (COBie).
  • Integrate only the sensor data that supports a defined operational use case.
  • Match twin fidelity to purpose — most value comes from simple sensor-to-model links.
  • Establish data governance and update processes to keep the twin 'live'.

Common Pitfalls

  • Calling a static as-built model a 'digital twin' with no live data.
  • Over-instrumenting before defining what decisions the twin supports.
  • No process to keep the twin synchronised, so it goes stale.
  • Ignoring cybersecurity of the connected OT/IoT data streams.
