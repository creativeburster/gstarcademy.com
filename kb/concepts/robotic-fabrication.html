Robotic Fabrication & CAD

Professional guide to robotic fabrication & cad — tools, workflows, software recommendations, and best practices for engineers and designers.

By Gstarcademy Editorial TeamUpdated 2026-06-30

Definition & Context

Robotic fabrication uses industrial robot arms for construction and manufacturing tasks — additive extrusion, milling, assembly, bricklaying, and finishing — programmed via CAD/CAM and parametric tools. It brings automation and complex, bespoke geometry to fabrication.

Toolchains like KUKA|prc and Grasshopper generate robot paths from geometry, with simulation for reach, singularities, and collisions before running the cell.

Why It Matters

Robotic fabrication automates bespoke, complex tasks that fixed machines can't, enabling mass-customisation and novel construction methods — provided reach, collisions, and process are simulated first.

Tools & Software

  • Grasshopper + KUKA|prc / Robots
  • RoboDK
  • robot OEM software (ABB RobotStudio)

Best Practices

  • Generate and simulate robot paths (reach, singularities, collisions).
  • Tune the process (extrusion, milling, welding) parameters to the task.
  • Design end-effectors and fixturing for the operation.
  • Validate in a digital twin before running the physical cell.

Common Pitfalls

  • Paths hitting reach limits, singularities, or collisions.
  • Process parameters not tuned to material/task.
  • Poor end-effector/fixture design.
  • Skipping simulation and crashing the cell.

Standards & Interoperability

  • robot safety (ISO 10218)
