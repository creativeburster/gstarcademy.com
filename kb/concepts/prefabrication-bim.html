Prefabrication & Modular Design in BIM

BIM for offsite construction: modular design, prefab panel detailing, logistics planning, and factory coordination.

By Gstarcademy Editorial Team Updated 2026-07-28 Editorial process

Definition & Context

Prefabrication and modular construction manufacture building elements — MEP racks, bathroom pods, wall panels, volumetric modules — off-site from the BIM model, then assemble on site. This demands high model accuracy (fabrication LOD 400), tight tolerance management, and coordination of connections and logistics.

BIM drives prefab through detailed shop models, clash-free coordination, quantity/fabrication data, and sequencing (4D) for transport and craning of modules.

Why It Matters

Prefab improves quality, safety, and schedule by moving work to a controlled factory — but only if the model is fabrication-accurate and tolerances/interfaces are rigorously coordinated.

Best Practices

  • Model to fabrication LOD with real connection and tolerance detail.
  • Coordinate interfaces between modules and with site-built work.
  • Plan transport/craning limits into module sizing and sequencing.
  • Use the model for fabrication data and factory scheduling.

Common Pitfalls

  • Design-LOD models used for fabrication, missing connection detail.
  • Tolerance/interface mismatches causing on-site fit problems.
  • Modules too large/heavy for transport or craning.
  • No 4D sequencing for delivery and assembly logistics.
