Multi Material 3Dprint

Professional guide to multi material 3dprint in CAD/BIM engineering workflows.

By Gstarcademy Editorial TeamUpdated 2026-06-30

Definition & Context

Multi-material 3-D printing deposits two or more materials in one build — combining rigid/flexible, different colours, or dissolvable supports — via technologies like PolyJet (Stratasys), material jetting, and multi-nozzle FFF. Design assigns materials per region and manages interface bonding and gradients.

Applications include over-moulded-like parts, living hinges, gradient stiffness, and realistic prototypes; digital materials blend base resins to hit target durometers and colours.

Why It Matters

Multi-material printing consolidates assemblies of different materials into one part — soft grips on rigid bodies, embedded seals, gradient properties — expanding what a single print can deliver.

Tools & Software

  • Stratasys GrabCAD (PolyJet)
  • multi-nozzle FFF slicers
  • material-assignment CAD

Best Practices

  • Assign materials per region and design robust material interfaces.
  • Use gradients/digital materials to tune stiffness and colour.
  • Design dissolvable supports for complex internal geometry.
  • Validate interface adhesion and durability for the use case.

Common Pitfalls

  • Weak bonding at dissimilar-material interfaces.
  • Abrupt property jumps causing stress/delamination.
  • Ignoring each material's shrinkage/process behaviour.
  • Over-using multi-material where single-material suffices.

Standards & Interoperability

  • material jetting / digital materials
