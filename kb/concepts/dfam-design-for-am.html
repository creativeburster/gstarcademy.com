Dfam Design For Am

Professional guide to dfam design for am in CAD/BIM engineering workflows.

By Gstarcademy Editorial TeamUpdated 2026-06-30

Definition & Context

Design for Additive Manufacturing (DfAM) is the set of principles for exploiting AM's freedoms while respecting its constraints: part consolidation, lightweighting via lattices/topology, self-supporting geometry, minimum feature sizes, and orientation-aware design. It reframes design around 'build it up' rather than 'cut it away'.

DfAM combines topology optimisation, lattice generation (nTop), and process rules (overhang angles, powder removal, support strategy) to produce parts that are lighter, consolidated, and manufacturable.

Why It Matters

AM only pays off when parts are designed for it. DfAM converts geometric freedom into real value — consolidation, weight savings, performance — while avoiding unbuildable or unnecessarily costly designs.

Tools & Software

  • nTopology
  • Autodesk Fusion (generative)
  • Materialise / Netfabb

Best Practices

  • Consolidate assemblies and add function only AM can deliver.
  • Design self-supporting angles and enable powder/support removal.
  • Use topology/lattices where they add real value, not gratuitously.
  • Design orientation-aware, respecting minimum features and finish.

Common Pitfalls

  • Porting a machined design to AM without redesign.
  • Trapped powder or inaccessible supports.
  • Lattices/complexity that add cost without benefit.
  • Ignoring minimum feature size and orientation effects.

Standards & Interoperability

  • ISO/ASTM 52900 DfAM guidance
