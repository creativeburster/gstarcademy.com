Parametric Design Principles

Core principles of parametric CAD: constraints, dimensions, feature history, and design intent capture.

By Gstarcademy Editorial Team Updated 2026-06-30 Editorial process

Definition & Context

Parametric design captures geometry as features driven by dimensions, constraints, and relations, so changing a parameter regenerates the model. The feature tree records design history; sketches are controlled by geometric constraints (coincident, parallel, tangent) and dimensional parameters, often linked by equations or a design table.

Robust parametric modelling follows design intent — building relationships so the model updates predictably. It contrasts with direct (history-free) modelling, which edits geometry without a feature tree.

Why It Matters

Parametric models let engineers explore variants and respond to change without rebuilding from scratch — the foundation of configurable products, families of parts, and fast design iteration.

Best Practices

  • Fully constrain sketches so geometry can't drift unexpectedly.
  • Build features in a logical order that reflects design intent.
  • Use equations/global variables to link related dimensions.
  • Reference stable geometry, avoiding fragile references that break on edit.

Common Pitfalls

  • Under-constrained sketches that move unpredictably when edited.
  • Fragile parent-child references causing rebuild errors ('feature failed').
  • Over-complex trees that are impossible to edit later.
  • Baking in numbers that should be driven parameters.
