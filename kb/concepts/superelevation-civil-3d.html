Superelevation

Civil 3D superelevation tools for calculating and applying road banking through curves according to design standards.

By Gstarcademy Editorial Team Updated 2026-07-28 Editorial process

Definition & Context

Superelevation in Civil 3D calculates and applies the cross-slope (banking) of a roadway through horizontal curves so vehicles can negotiate them safely at design speed. It derives rates from a chosen design standard (e.g., AASHTO) based on curve radius and design speed, and applies runoff/runout transitions through the alignment's superelevation data, which the corridor then builds.

Superelevation is stored on the alignment and drives corridor cross-sections; critical stations mark where full super, runoff, and runout occur.

Why It Matters

Superelevation is a safety- and standards-critical geometric element; deriving it from the design standard ensures curves are banked correctly and consistently with agency criteria.

Because the corridor reads the alignment's superelevation, getting the standard, speed, and radius right is what makes the built model code-compliant.

Best Practices

  • Choose the correct design standard, design speed, and e-max for the road class
  • Review critical stations (runoff/runout) so transitions meet the standard
  • Rebuild the corridor after editing superelevation so sections reflect banking
  • Verify the pivot method and lane/shoulder treatment match agency criteria

Common Pitfalls

  • Wrong design speed or standard giving non-compliant banking
  • Ignoring runoff/runout transitions, creating abrupt cross-slope changes
  • Corridor not rebuilt after superelevation edits
