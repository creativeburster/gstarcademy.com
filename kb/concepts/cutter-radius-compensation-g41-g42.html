Cutter Radius Compensation (G41, G42 & G40)

Complete reference for G41 and G42 cutter radius compensation. Learn how to achieve precise dimensional tolerances on CNC milling and turning machines.

By Gstarcademy Editorial Team Updated 2026-10-12 Editorial process

1. Definition & Engineering Significance

Cutter Radius Compensation (CRC), commanded via G41 (cutter left of programmed path) and G42 (cutter right of programmed path) with cancellation via G40, is the industry-standard CNC programming method for maintaining exact part dimensions. In 2D profile contouring and turning, CAM software generates toolpaths based on a nominal tool diameter. On the shop floor, slight cutter wear, tool deflection, or variations in reground endmill sizes require fine adjustments to maintain tight engineering tolerances (e.g., $\pm 0.005$ mm).

2. Technical Architecture & Key Parameters

  • G41 (Climb Milling / Left Offset): Offsets the cutter to the left of the programmed contour relative to the direction of tool motion. Standard for climb milling on modern rigid vertical machining centers.
  • G42 (Conventional Milling / Right Offset): Offsets the cutter to the right of the programmed contour. Used in conventional milling or specialized internal turning bore profiles.
  • G40 (Compensation Cancel): Cancels cutter compensation. Must always be executed along a linear lead-out move away from the finished wall to prevent gouging.
  • Wear Mode vs Control Mode: In 'Wear' mode, CAM programs the true part contour and the operator enters small wear deltas in the CNC offset register (e.g., -0.02mm). In 'Control' mode, CAM outputs the part edge and the operator inputs the full tool radius.

3. Industry Best Practices & Failure Modes

A critical engineering failure in CNC programming is improper lead-in/lead-out geometry when activating cutter compensation. Engaging G41 on a motion block that is shorter than the cutter radius, or engaging it directly into an internal corner, triggers controller alarm 'Interference in Cutter Radius Compensation' or produces severe overcut gouges.

4. Production Governance & Standard Protocols

Best practice involves programming tangential linear lead-ins longer than the cutter radius before entering finished part contours. By mastering G41/G42 wear offsets, CNC machinists can dial in precision bearing press fits and O-ring gland depths on the machine control without returning to the CAM desk to re-post code.
