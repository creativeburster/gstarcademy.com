Process Plant Piping Design

Professional guide to process plant piping design — tools, workflows, software recommendations, and best practices for engineers and designers.

By Gstarcademy Editorial TeamUpdated 2026-06-30

Definition & Context

Process-plant piping design engineers the piping systems of chemical, oil & gas, and industrial facilities to codes such as ASME B31.3. It spans line sizing (hydraulics), material selection for service, wall-thickness/pressure design, flexibility/stress analysis, and support design, all driven from P&IDs.

Integrated plant tools (AVEVA E3D, SmartPlant, Plant 3D) maintain data-consistent P&ID-to-3-D models, and CAESAR II handles thermal-flexibility and load-case analysis for compliance.

Why It Matters

Process piping carries hazardous fluids at pressure and temperature. Code-compliant material, wall-thickness, and stress design are safety-critical and legally required.

Tools & Software

  • AVEVA E3D / SmartPlant
  • AutoCAD Plant 3D
  • CAESAR II (stress)

Best Practices

  • Select materials and wall thickness for the service and code.
  • Perform flexibility/stress analysis for thermal and load cases.
  • Keep 3-D model, P&IDs, and line list data-consistent.
  • Design supports, and comply with B31.3 throughout.

Common Pitfalls

  • Wrong material/wall thickness for pressure/temperature/service.
  • Skipping flexibility analysis, risking overstress at nozzles.
  • P&ID/3-D data drift causing errors.
  • Inadequate supports and thermal-expansion provision.

Standards & Interoperability

  • ASME B31.3
  • material/service specs
