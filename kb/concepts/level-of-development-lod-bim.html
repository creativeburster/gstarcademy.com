BIM Level of Development (LOD 100 to LOD 500)

The standardized framework defined by BIMForum and AIA detailing the geometric completeness and non-graphic reliability of building elements across design phases.

By Gstarcademy Editorial Team Updated 2026-09-30 Editorial process

1. Definition & Engineering Significance

LOD prevents over-modeling and protects project liability by defining how much an engineer can rely on a 3D model component at each stage of a project: LOD 100 (Conceptual mass), LOD 200 (Generic system), LOD 300 (Specific dimensions and location), LOD 350 (Interfacing details), LOD 400 (Fabrication/CNC ready), and LOD 500 (Field-verified as-built).

2. Key Technical Characteristics & Rules

  • LOD vs LOD: Differentiates Level of Detail (visual quantity of geometry) from Level of Development (contractual reliability of information).
  • LOD 350 Importance: Essential for clash detection; includes connection plates, rebar clearances, and support bracket geometry.
  • BIM Execution Plan (BEP) Integration: Specifies contractual LOD milestones at schematic, detailed design, and construction handover.

3. BIMForum LOD Framework Progression

The Level of Development (LOD) framework standardizes model completeness across project milestones:

  • LOD 100 (Conceptual): Schematic 3D masses conveying overall spatial volume, orientation, and cost metrics.
  • LOD 200 (Approximate Geometry): Generic systems or assemblies with approximate size, shape, and location.
  • LOD 300 (Design Coordination): Specific architectural/structural elements suitable for regulatory submittals and trade coordination.
  • LOD 350 (Field Interfaces): Includes interface connections, embeds, and support brackets necessary to resolve cross-trade clashes.
  • LOD 400 (Fabrication): Precise shop fabrication assemblies with weld preparations, bolt patterns, and assembly sequencing.

4. Contractual Governance & Model Bloat Avoidance

A frequent contracting trap is demanding global LOD 400 or 500 across an entire project. This drastically inflates authoring costs, multiplies file sizes, and overwhelms coordinator workstations. BIM Execution Plans must define an LOD progression matrix that assigns high LOD only to critical congested zones (e.g., MEP plant rooms).
