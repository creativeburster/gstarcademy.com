Subscription vs Perpetual Licensing

CAD software licensing models compared: subscription benefits, perpetual license advantages, and total cost of ownership analysis.

By Gstarcademy Editorial Team Updated 2026-07-28 Editorial process

Definition & Context

CAD licensing has shifted from perpetual (buy once, own the version, optional maintenance) to subscription (term-based rental including updates and support). Many vendors (Autodesk) have ended new perpetual sales; others (BricsCAD, Rhino) still offer perpetual licences. Licensing models include named-user, network/floating, and token-based.

The choice affects cash flow (capex vs opex), always-current software, license mobility, and long-term total cost of ownership.

Why It Matters

Licensing model drives budgeting, compliance, and flexibility. Understanding named-user vs network licensing and TCO over several years prevents both overspending and compliance risk.

Best Practices

  • Compare multi-year TCO, not just first-year price, for each model.
  • Match license type (named vs network) to how staff actually work.
  • Track entitlements and usage to stay compliant and avoid waste.
  • Consider perpetual-friendly vendors where long-term stability matters.

Common Pitfalls

  • Comparing only year-one cost and ignoring multi-year TCO.
  • Buying named-user seats for occasional/shared users (network fits better).
  • Assuming perpetual licences still get updates without maintenance.
  • License non-compliance from untracked installs.
