Offshore Platform Design

Professional guide to offshore platform design — tools, workflows, software recommendations, and best practices for engineers and designers.

By Gstarcademy Editorial TeamUpdated 2026-06-30

Definition & Context

Offshore-platform design engineers fixed (jacket, gravity) and floating (semi-sub, FPSO, spar, TLP) structures for oil & gas and offshore wind, resisting waves, current, wind, and (where relevant) ice, plus fatigue over decades. It integrates hydrodynamics, structural analysis, and marine operations (installation, mooring).

Tools include SACS, SESAM, and OrcaFlex for structural/hydrodynamic and mooring analysis, with 3-D plant/structure modelling for topsides and rigorous fatigue and code (API, DNV) compliance.

Why It Matters

Offshore structures face extreme, cyclic marine environments where failure is catastrophic and remote. Wave loading, fatigue, and installation engineering under strict class rules are paramount.

Tools & Software

  • Bentley SACS / DNV SESAM
  • Orcina OrcaFlex (mooring)
  • 3-D plant modelling

Best Practices

  • Analyse wave/wind/current loads and long-term fatigue.
  • Design mooring/foundations for the site metocean conditions.
  • Engineer installation and marine operations, not just in-place.
  • Comply with API/DNV offshore codes throughout.

Common Pitfalls

  • Underestimating fatigue over decades of wave cycles.
  • Ignoring installation/transport load cases.
  • Mooring/foundation not matched to metocean data.
  • Non-compliance with class (API/DNV) requirements.

Standards & Interoperability

  • API RP 2A / DNV offshore
