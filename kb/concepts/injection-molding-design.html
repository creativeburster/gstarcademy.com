Injection Molding Design Guidelines

Designing plastic parts for injection molding: draft, wall thickness, ribs, bosses, gates, and sink marks.

By Gstarcademy Editorial Team Updated 2026-07-28 Editorial process

Definition & Context

Injection-moulding design shapes plastic parts for the mould-and-inject cycle: uniform wall thickness, draft on all vertical faces, ribs (≤60% of wall thickness to avoid sink), bosses with support, and generous radii. Gate location, parting line, and ejection are planned with the toolmaker.

Moldflow and similar tools simulate fill, pack, cooling, and warpage to predict short shots, weld lines, air traps, and sink marks — driving gate placement and wall/rib tuning before steel is cut.

Why It Matters

Mould tooling is expensive and slow to change. Following moulding rules and simulating flow up front prevents cosmetic and structural defects and shortens cycle time, directly affecting unit cost.

Best Practices

  • Keep walls uniform; make ribs ≤60% of wall thickness to prevent sink.
  • Add draft (≥1°) to all faces along the pull direction.
  • Simulate fill/warp to place gates and avoid weld lines in critical areas.
  • Design in cooling and ejection with the toolmaker early.

Common Pitfalls

  • Thick or non-uniform walls causing sink marks and long cycle times.
  • Zero/insufficient draft making ejection damage the part.
  • Ribs too thick, producing visible sink on the show surface.
  • Ignoring weld-line placement in structurally/cosmetically critical zones.
