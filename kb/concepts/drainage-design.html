Drainage System Design

Professional guide to drainage system design — tools, workflows, software recommendations, and best practices for engineers and designers.

By Gstarcademy Editorial TeamUpdated 2026-06-30

Definition & Context

Drainage design conveys and manages surface and subsurface water for roads, sites, and buildings — gullies, pipes, culverts, channels, and outfalls — sized by hydrology (rainfall intensity, catchment) and hydraulics (Manning's, HGL/EGL) for the design storm.

Civil tools (Civil 3D + Storm and Sanitary Analysis, InfoDrainage, Bentley OpenFlows) model gravity networks, check surcharge/flooding, and integrate SUDS for water quality and attenuation.

Why It Matters

Poor drainage causes flooding, road failure, and erosion. Correct hydrological sizing and hydraulic checks protect infrastructure and meet discharge/water-quality regulations.

Tools & Software

  • Autodesk SSA / InfoDrainage
  • Bentley OpenFlows
  • Civil 3D pipe networks

Best Practices

  • Compute runoff for the required design-storm return periods.
  • Size pipes/culverts/channels and check the hydraulic grade line.
  • Incorporate SUDS/attenuation for quality and discharge limits.
  • Design outfalls and erosion protection appropriately.

Common Pitfalls

  • Under-sizing for design storm, causing surcharge/flooding.
  • Ignoring tailwater/HGL and outfall conditions.
  • No attenuation, exceeding allowable discharge.
  • Neglecting maintenance access to the network.

Standards & Interoperability

  • local drainage/SUDS standards
