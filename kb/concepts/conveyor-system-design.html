Conveyor System Design

Professional guide to conveyor system design — tools, workflows, software recommendations, and best practices for engineers and designers.

By GstarCAD Academy Engineering Editorial Board (Suzhou Gstarsoft Co., Ltd.) Reviewed by GstarCAD Academy Editorial Team Last reviewed 2026-08-16 Editorial Process

Definition & Context

Conveyor system design engineers material-handling systems — belt, roller, chain, screw — sizing belts/chains, drives, and supports for the throughput, material, and layout. Belt conveyors follow CEMA methods for capacity, tension, power, and pulley/idler selection.

Layout is modelled in CAD (often with manufacturer libraries), integrating drives, take-ups, transfers, and safety guarding; power and tension calculations drive component selection.

Why It Matters

Conveyors are the backbone of bulk and unit material handling. Correct capacity, tension, and drive sizing ensure reliable throughput without belt slip, overload, or spillage.

Tools & Software

  • AutoCAD/Inventor (layout)
  • CEMA calculation tools
  • manufacturer selection software

Best Practices

  • Size capacity, belt tension, and drive power per CEMA methods.
  • Select idlers, pulleys, and take-up for the tension and material.
  • Design transfers to minimise spillage and belt wear.
  • Include guarding, access, and maintenance in the layout.

Common Pitfalls

  • Undersized drive/tension causing belt slip or stall.
  • Poor transfer design causing spillage and wear.
  • Ignoring material characteristics (angle of repose, abrasion).
  • Inadequate guarding/maintenance access.

Standards & Interoperability

  • CEMA belt conveyor standards
  • Eurocode 7 (Geotechnical Design - EN 1997)
  • ISO 19650 (BIM for Civil Infrastructure)
