Cable Harness Design

Professional guide to cable harness design — tools, workflows, software recommendations, and best practices for engineers and designers.

By Gstarcademy Editorial TeamUpdated 2026-06-30

Definition & Context

Cable/wire-harness design engineers the bundled wiring that connects a product's electrical components — routing, wire selection, connectors, terminals, and protection. It bridges electrical (schematic/connectivity) and mechanical (3-D routing, bend radius, clips) domains, producing flattened formboard drawings for manufacture.

Tools include SOLIDWORKS Electrical/Routing, Siemens Capital, and Zuken E3.series, which link the logical schematic to the 3-D harness and generate wire lists, lengths, and formboards.

Why It Matters

Harnesses are labour-intensive and error-prone. Linking electrical connectivity to accurate 3-D routing yields correct wire lengths, bend radii, and manufacturable formboards, cutting rework.

Tools & Software

  • SOLIDWORKS Electrical/Routing
  • Siemens Capital
  • Zuken E3.series

Best Practices

  • Drive the 3-D harness from the electrical schematic/connectivity.
  • Respect wire bend radius, routing, and protection/clip placement.
  • Generate accurate wire lists, lengths, and flattened formboards.
  • Coordinate harness routing with the mechanical packaging.

Common Pitfalls

  • Disconnected electrical and mechanical data causing errors.
  • Wrong wire lengths from ignoring 3-D routing/slack.
  • Bend-radius/clip violations damaging wires.
  • Formboards that don't match the assembled product.

Standards & Interoperability

  • IPC/WHMA-A-620 (harness)
