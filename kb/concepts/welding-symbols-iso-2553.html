Welding and Brazing Symbols (ISO 2553 / AWS A2.4)

Symbolic drafting system for specifying weld type, throat thickness, joint preparation, non-destructive testing, and field execution requirements.

By Gstarcademy Editorial Team Updated 2026-09-30 Editorial process

1. Definition & Engineering Significance

ISO 2553 eliminates extensive textual annotations on structural steel and pressure vessel drawings. A reference line, arrow line, and elementary symbol clearly designate fillet welds, butt welds, plug welds, and back-gouging commands.

2. Key Technical Characteristics & Rules

  • Arrow Side vs Other Side: Symbols placed directly on the continuous reference line apply to the arrow side; symbols on the dashed identification line apply to the other side.
  • Throat Thickness (a) vs Leg Length (z): ISO specifies effective throat thickness (a-dimension); American AWS callouts commonly denote leg length (z-dimension).
  • Field Weld Flag: A flag placed at the junction of arrow and reference line denotes welding to be performed on site rather than in the fabrication shop.

3. Arrow Side, Other Side & Dual Reference Lines

ISO 2553 defines a rigorous graphical shorthand to communicate weld types, sizes, and inspection requirements on fabrication drawings:

  • Arrow Side vs. Other Side: Weld symbols placed on the solid reference line apply to the side pointed to by the arrow; symbols placed on the dashed identification line apply to the opposite side.
  • Fillet Weld Dimensions: Leg length is designated with prefix 'z' (e.g., z8), while design throat thickness is designated with prefix 'a' (e.g., a6).
  • Peripheral & Field Welds: A circle at the reference line elbow indicates an all-around weld; a black flag symbol indicates an on-site field weld.

4. Structural Safety & Drawing Ambiguity Risks

Confusing throat thickness ('a') with leg length ('z') will undersize the physical weld by approximately 30%, risking catastrophic structural joint failure under dynamic fatigue loads. Quality assurance protocols mandate verifying that fabricators interpret dual reference lines according to the specified ISO 2553 standard version.
