Fatigue Analysis in CAE

Fatigue life prediction in CAE: S-N curves, strain-life approach, cycle counting, and critical plane methods.

By Gstarcademy Editorial Team Updated 2026-06-30 Editorial process

Definition & Context

Fatigue analysis predicts crack initiation and life under cyclic loading, where a component fails far below its static strength. The two dominant approaches are stress-life (S-N) for high-cycle fatigue (>10⁴ cycles, elastic) using Wöhler curves, and strain-life (ε-N) for low-cycle fatigue where local plasticity dominates.

Variable-amplitude histories are reduced to cycle counts with rainflow counting, then accumulated with Miner's rule (Σ nᵢ/Nᵢ = 1 at failure). Mean-stress corrections (Goodman, Gerber, Soderberg) account for the damaging effect of tensile mean stress. Tools include nCode DesignLife, ANSYS nCode, fe-safe, and FEMFAT.

Why It Matters

Most in-service mechanical failures are fatigue, not overload. Getting fatigue life right governs warranty, safety, and weight — over-design wastes material, under-design causes field failures.

Best Practices

  • Use rainflow counting on real measured or simulated load histories, not a single peak load.
  • Apply the correct mean-stress correction — Goodman is conservative for brittle materials, Gerber less so for ductile.
  • Account for surface finish, size, and stress-concentration (Kf) factors that dramatically reduce life.
  • Use strain-life for low-cycle/thermal-mechanical fatigue where plasticity occurs.
  • Correlate against test data or field returns to calibrate the damage model.

Common Pitfalls

  • Applying an S-N (high-cycle) method to a low-cycle plastic problem.
  • Ignoring mean stress — tensile mean stress can halve life.
  • Using idealised-smooth S-N data without surface-finish and notch knockdown factors.
  • Assuming Miner's linear damage is exact; sequence effects can matter.
