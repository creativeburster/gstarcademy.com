Cam Mechanism Design

Professional guide to cam mechanism design — tools, workflows, software recommendations, and best practices for engineers and designers.

By Gstarcademy Editorial TeamUpdated 2026-06-30

Definition & Context

Cam mechanism design shapes a cam profile to drive a follower through a specified displacement–time (motion) programme. The cam profile derives from the chosen motion law (cycloidal, modified-trapezoidal, polynomial) that controls velocity, acceleration, and jerk to avoid shock and vibration at speed.

Design tools generate the profile from the motion diagram and check pressure angle and radius of curvature; the profile is then machined (often 5-axis or wire EDM) to tight tolerance.

Why It Matters

Cam motion laws determine dynamic behaviour — poor laws cause vibration, noise, and follower jump at speed. Correct profile synthesis delivers smooth, high-speed motion.

Tools & Software

  • cam design software
  • SOLIDWORKS/Inventor (profile)
  • motion analysis tools

Best Practices

  • Select a motion law that limits acceleration and jerk for the speed.
  • Keep the pressure angle within limits to avoid side thrust.
  • Ensure the radius of curvature exceeds the follower radius (no undercut).
  • Maintain follower preload/force closure to prevent jump.

Common Pitfalls

  • Motion laws with discontinuous acceleration causing shock.
  • Excessive pressure angle producing high side loads.
  • Undercutting from too-small curvature radius.
  • Follower jump at speed from inadequate preload.
