Bim For Renovation

Professional guide to bim for renovation in CAD/BIM engineering workflows.

By Gstarcademy Editorial TeamUpdated 2026-06-30

Definition & Context

Renovation/retrofit BIM starts from existing conditions captured by laser scanning/photogrammetry (scan-to-BIM), then models proposed alterations, phasing, and demolition. It manages uncertainty in existing structures and services and supports clash-checking new work against as-found conditions.

Revit phasing (existing/demo/new), point-cloud underlays, and coordination tools track what stays, goes, and is added, informing structural interventions and service replacements.

Why It Matters

Retrofit dominates construction in mature markets. Accurate existing-condition models and clear phasing prevent the nasty surprises — hidden structure, mis-measured spaces — that plague renovation projects.

Tools & Software

  • Autodesk ReCap (point cloud)
  • Revit (phasing)
  • Navisworks (coordination)

Best Practices

  • Base the model on accurate scan/survey of existing conditions.
  • Use phasing (existing/demo/new) to communicate scope clearly.
  • Clash-check new work against as-found structure and services.
  • Model to the LOD the intervention actually requires.

Common Pitfalls

  • Designing against inaccurate or assumed existing conditions.
  • Unclear phasing/demolition scope causing site confusion.
  • Ignoring hidden structure/services behind finishes.
  • Over-modelling existing fabric that isn't affected.

Standards & Interoperability

  • scan-to-BIM workflows
