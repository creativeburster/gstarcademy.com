Topology Optimization Am

Professional guide to topology optimization am in CAD/BIM engineering workflows.

By Gstarcademy Editorial TeamUpdated 2026-06-30

Definition & Context

Topology optimisation for AM computationally removes material from a design space, keeping only what's needed to carry loads — producing organic, high-efficiency structures ideally suited to additive manufacture. It uses methods like SIMP density optimisation under stiffness/stress and manufacturing constraints.

Tools (Altair Inspire/OptiStruct, Fusion Generative, nTop, Ansys) generate optimised geometry that is then smoothed/rebuilt and validated by FEA before printing.

Why It Matters

Topology optimisation plus AM delivers dramatic weight and material savings for aerospace, automotive, and medical parts — geometry too organic to machine but ideal to print.

Tools & Software

  • Altair Inspire / OptiStruct
  • Autodesk Fusion (generative)
  • nTopology
  • Ansys

Best Practices

  • Define load cases, constraints, and keep-out regions accurately.
  • Apply AM/manufacturing constraints (overhang, min member size).
  • Reconstruct/smooth the raw result into a clean, printable model.
  • Validate the optimised part with FEA before committing.

Common Pitfalls

  • Optimising against incomplete/incorrect load cases.
  • Printing raw optimisation output without cleanup/validation.
  • Geometry impossible to build even with AM constraints.
  • Ignoring stress concentrations in the smoothed result.

Standards & Interoperability

  • topology optimisation methods (SIMP)
