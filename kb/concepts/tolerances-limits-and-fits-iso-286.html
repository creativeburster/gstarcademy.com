ISO System of Limits and Fits (ISO 286)

The international standard code defining hole-basis and shaft-basis tolerance classes, fundamental deviations, and standard tolerance grades (IT).

By GstarCAD Academy Engineering Editorial Board (Suzhou Gstarsoft Co., Ltd.) Reviewed by GstarCAD Academy Editorial Team Last reviewed 2026-09-30 Editorial Process & Testbed

1. Definition & Engineering Significance

ISO 286 categorizes shaft and hole fits into Clearance, Transition, and Interference fits. By specifying standardized alphanumeric codes (such as H7/g6 for precision running fits or H7/p6 for press fits), engineers avoid arbitrary decimal dimensioning.

2. Key Technical Characteristics & Rules

  • Hole Basis System: The basic hole size has a lower deviation of zero (symbol H). Variations in shaft size dictate the resulting fit nature.
  • Fundamental Deviation Letters: Capital letters denote internal features (holes A–ZC); lowercase letters denote external features (shafts a–zc).
  • Standard Tolerance Grade (IT): IT01 to IT18 grades define tolerance magnitude scaling proportionally with nominal dimension.

3. Hole-Basis & Shaft-Basis Fit Categories

ISO 286 provides a standardized system of fundamental tolerance grades (IT01 to IT18) and fundamental deviations (letters A-ZC for holes, a-zc for shafts):

  • Clearance Fits (e.g., H7/g6, H7/f7): Guarantees positive clearance between mating parts for sliding shafts, rotating bearings, and free axial guides.
  • Transition Fits (e.g., H7/k6, H7/m6): May produce slight clearance or slight interference, used for precision rigid location with light mallet assembly.
  • Interference Fits (e.g., H7/p6, H7/s6): Guarantees negative clearance, creating heavy friction joints for press-fit bushings and gears requiring thermal shrink fitting.

4. Hole-Basis System Standard Operating Procedure

Standard manufacturing practice dictates using the Hole-Basis system (specifying hole tolerance H7), because standard reamers, drills, and plug gages are manufactured to fixed H sizes. Shafts can be turned and ground flexibly to arbitrary tolerance classes on standard lathes.
