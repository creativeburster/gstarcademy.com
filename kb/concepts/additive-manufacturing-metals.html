Additive Manufacturing Metals

Professional guide to additive manufacturing metals in CAD/BIM engineering workflows.

By Gstarcademy Editorial TeamUpdated 2026-06-30

Definition & Context

Metal additive manufacturing builds parts layer-by-layer from powder or wire — laser powder-bed fusion (LPBF/SLM), electron-beam melting (EBM), directed energy deposition (DED), and binder jetting. Design leverages complex geometry (conformal cooling, consolidation) while managing supports, residual stress, and orientation.

Preparation tools (Materialise Magics, Netfabb, nTop) handle orientation, supports, and build-plate layout; post-processing (heat treatment, HIP, machining) is integral to achieving properties and tolerances.

Why It Matters

Metal AM enables geometry impossible to machine or cast — consolidated assemblies, conformal cooling, lightweight lattices — but demands design for supports, residual stress, and post-processing to be viable.

Tools & Software

  • Materialise Magics
  • Autodesk Netfabb
  • nTopology
  • machine OEM software

Best Practices

  • Orient parts to manage residual stress, supports, and surface finish.
  • Design self-supporting angles and minimise support on critical faces.
  • Plan post-processing (stress relief, HIP, machining) into the design.
  • Account for anisotropy and validate properties for the process.

Common Pitfalls

  • Ignoring residual stress, causing warping/cracking.
  • Excessive/inaccessible supports raising cost and finishing.
  • Assuming as-built tolerance/finish without machining allowance.
  • Treating AM properties as identical to wrought material.

Standards & Interoperability

  • ASTM/ISO 52900 series
