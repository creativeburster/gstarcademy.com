Johnson-Cook Constitutive Model for High-Strain Dynamic Plasticity

Mathematical formulation and parameter calibration of the Johnson-Cook material strength and damage model in explicit FEA (LS-DYNA, Abaqus, ANSYS).

1. Definition & Mathematical Engineering Foundations

The Johnson-Cook constitutive model is the premier phenomenological viscoplastic material model used in computational mechanics and explicit finite element analysis (FEA) to simulate metal deformation under high strain rates, large plastic strains, and high thermal softening. Standardized for ballistic projectile impacts, automotive crash simulations, and high-speed CNC metal cutting mechanics, it decouples strain hardening, strain rate sensitivity, and thermal softening into an elegant, computationally stable equation.

2. Technical Architecture & Key Formulations

  • Flow Stress Equation ($\sigma_y$):
    σy = [A + B · εpn] · [1 + C · ln(ε̇*)] · [1 − (T*)m]
    where $\varepsilon_p$ is effective plastic strain, $\dot{\varepsilon}^* = \dot{\varepsilon} / \dot{\varepsilon}_0$ is normalized strain rate, and $T^* = (T - T_{\text{room}}) / (T_{\text{melt}} - T_{\text{room}})$ is homologous temperature.
  • A (Initial Yield Stress): Yield strength of the material at reference strain rate and room temperature.
  • B & n (Strain Hardening Modulus & Exponent): Captures work hardening behavior during large plastic deformation.
  • C (Strain Rate Sensitivity Coefficient): Governs flow stress elevation under high strain velocity (Hopkinson bar impact conditions).
  • m (Thermal Softening Exponent): Controls progressive strength drop as adiabatic cutting shear heat approaches material melting temperature.

3. Industry Failure Modes & Risk Mitigation

In CNC machining chip formation simulations, using standard static elasto-plastic models causes severe mesh distortion errors because the solver fails to account for the extreme temperatures ($>800^\circ\text{C}$) and strain rates ($>10^5\,\text{s}^{-1}$) occurring in the primary shear zone.

4. Production Governance & Metrological Standards

Explicit FEA solvers (LS-DYNA, Abaqus/Explicit) pair the Johnson-Cook strength equation with the Johnson-Cook Failure/Damage criterion ($D = \sum \Delta \varepsilon_p / \varepsilon_f$), automatically deleting sheared elements to simulate chip breaking, metal armor perforation, and explosive metal forming.
