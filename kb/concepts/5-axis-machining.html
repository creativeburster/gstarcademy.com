5-Axis CNC Machining

5-axis simultaneous machining strategies: swarf cutting, multi-axis contouring, and collision avoidance in CAM.

By Gstarcademy Editorial Team Updated 2026-06-30 Editorial process

Definition & Context

5-axis machining adds two rotary axes to the three linear axes, letting the tool approach the workpiece from almost any orientation. 3+2 (positional) machining locks the rotaries at fixed angles for each face, while simultaneous 5-axis moves all axes together to machine complex contoured surfaces (impellers, blades, molds) in one setup.

Benefits include fewer setups, shorter tools (more rigidity, better finish), and access to undercuts. Programming requires careful control of tool-axis vectors, collision/gouge avoidance, and often kinematic-aware posts (TCP/RTCP).

Why It Matters

5-axis cuts setups and enables geometry impossible on 3-axis machines, improving accuracy and finish. It is essential for aerospace, mould, and turbomachinery work.

Best Practices

  • Use 3+2 for prismatic faces; reserve simultaneous 5-axis for true contoured surfaces.
  • Use shorter tools enabled by tilt for rigidity and finish.
  • Run full machine-kinematic collision/gouge checks before cutting.
  • Ensure the post supports TCPC/RTCP for your control.

Common Pitfalls

  • Programming simultaneous motion where 3+2 would be simpler and safer.
  • Collisions from holder/rotary interference not checked in simulation.
  • Tool-axis vectors causing gouges on steep/undercut regions.
  • Post lacking proper tool-center-point handling.
