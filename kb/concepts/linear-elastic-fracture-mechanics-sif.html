Linear Elastic Fracture Mechanics (LEFM): Stress Intensity Factor (K) & Fracture Toughness

Foundations of Linear Elastic Fracture Mechanics (LEFM). Calculate Mode I Stress Intensity Factor K_I, critical fracture toughness K_IC, and crack propagation.

1. Definition & Mathematical Engineering Foundations

Linear Elastic Fracture Mechanics (LEFM), established by Griffith and Irwin, is the engineering discipline that predicts the catastrophic failure of cracked structural components under applied mechanical stress. In contrast to conventional yield-stress failure theories (which assume unblemished continuum materials), LEFM acknowledges that all real-world welded bridges, pressure vessels, and aircraft skins contain microscopic cracks, pores, or metallurgical inclusions. Failure occurs when the crack-tip stress intensity factor ($K$) exceeds the material's plane-strain fracture toughness ($K_{IC}$).

2. Technical Architecture & Key Formulations

  • Three Classical Fracture Modes: Mode I (Opening): Tensile stress normal to the crack plane (most ubiquitous and dangerous in engineering). Mode II (Sliding): In-plane shear. Mode III (Tearing): Out-of-plane anti-plane shear.
  • Mode I Stress Intensity Factor ($K_I$):
    KI = Y · σ · √(π · a)
    where $\sigma$ is nominal tensile stress, $a$ is crack half-length, and $Y$ is a dimensionless geometry correction factor.
  • Fracture Toughness Criterion ($K_{IC}$): Catastrophic unstable fracture occurs without warning when:
    KI ≥ KIC
    $K_{IC}$ is an intrinsic material property (e.g., $100\,\text{MPa}\sqrt{\text{m}}$ for structural steel; $25\,\text{MPa}\sqrt{\text{m}}$ for high-strength aluminum 7075-T6).
  • Paris Law Crack Propagation ($da/dN$):
    da / dN = C · (ΔK)m
    Predicts structural fatigue lifespan under cyclic loading before critical crack size is reached.

3. Industry Failure Modes & Risk Mitigation

The catastrophic historical collapse of Liberty ships in WWII and the Comet jet airliners resulted from ignoring LEFM: stress concentrations around square cargo hatch corners initiated small cracks that rapidly achieved critical size ($K_I = K_{IC}$), causing immediate hull splitting in cold seawater.

4. Production Governance & Metrological Standards

FEA fracture analysis tools in ANSYS and Abaqus compute J-integrals and contour stress intensity factors around crack tips, enabling non-destructive testing (NDT) teams to specify maximum allowable inspection flaw sizes per ASME Boiler & Pressure Vessel Code (BPVC Section XI).
