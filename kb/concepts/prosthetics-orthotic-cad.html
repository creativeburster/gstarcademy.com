Prosthetics & Orthotics CAD

Professional guide to prosthetics & orthotics cad — tools, workflows, software recommendations, and best practices for engineers and designers.

By Gstarcademy Editorial TeamUpdated 2026-06-30

Definition & Context

Prosthetic and orthotic (P&O) CAD designs custom limbs, sockets, braces, and insoles from a 3-D scan of the patient's anatomy. Digital workflows (Rodin4D, Vorum, Standard Cyborg) replace plaster casting with scan-based modelling, rectification, and CAM/AM fabrication for a personalised, repeatable fit.

Design balances fit and comfort (pressure distribution, relief of bony prominences) with structural strength, using scan → rectify → design → mill/print workflows and biocompatible materials.

Why It Matters

Custom P&O devices must fit unique anatomy comfortably and safely. Digital scan-to-CAD/CAM workflows deliver better fit, repeatability, and faster iteration than traditional plaster methods.

Tools & Software

  • Rodin4D / Vorum (P&O CAD)
  • Standard Cyborg
  • scan + CAM/AM fabrication

Best Practices

  • Base the device on an accurate anatomical scan.
  • Rectify for pressure relief over bony prominences and comfort.
  • Ensure structural strength and durability for daily loads.
  • Use biocompatible materials and validate fit with the patient.

Common Pitfalls

  • Poor scan or rectification causing discomfort/pressure sores.
  • Under-strength devices failing under daily loading.
  • Ignoring biocompatibility of skin-contact materials.
  • No fit validation/iteration with the patient.

Standards & Interoperability

  • ISO 13485
  • biocompatibility (ISO 10993)
