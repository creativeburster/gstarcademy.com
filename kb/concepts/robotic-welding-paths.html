Robotic Welding Paths

Offline-programmed torch paths for automated welding: orientation, sequencing, seam tracking, and collision-checked simulation.

By Gstarcademy Editorial TeamUpdated 2026-06-30

Definition & Context

Robotic welding path programming defines torch trajectories, orientations, and weld parameters for automated welding. Offline programming (OLP) in CAD/CAM (Delmia, RobotStudio, Octopuz) generates and simulates paths from the weld model, optimising torch angle, approach, and cycle time and avoiding collisions.

Seam finding/tracking (touch sensing, laser) compensates for part variation, while parameter schedules control heat input, penetration, and distortion.

Why It Matters

Offline-programmed robotic welding delivers consistent, high-throughput welds and frees the cell from teach-time. Correct torch angles, sequencing, and seam tracking ensure weld quality and low distortion.

The value of OLP is that programming and simulation happen on the digital model while the cell keeps producing — but it only pays off if the virtual cell matches reality, so the robot, fixtures, and part must be accurately calibrated or the offline paths miss the seam. Two factors dominate quality: weld sequencing (alternating and balancing welds to steer thermal distortion rather than warping the assembly) and seam finding/tracking (touch sensing or through-arc/laser tracking) that compensates for part-to-part variation the ideal CAD model never shows.

Tools & Software

  • Dassault DELMIA
  • ABB RobotStudio
  • Octopuz (OLP)

Best Practices

  • Program torch angle/orientation and approach for each seam.
  • Sequence welds to control distortion and residual stress.
  • Use seam finding/tracking to handle part variation.
  • Simulate for collisions/reach and validate weld parameters.

Common Pitfalls

  • Poor torch angles causing defects/lack of fusion.
  • Weld sequencing that warps the assembly.
  • No seam tracking, so part variation ruins welds.
  • Collisions/reach issues from unsimulated paths.

Standards & Interoperability

  • Weld procedures and symbols follow AWS D1.1 / ISO 3834 and ISO 2553 weld-symbol conventions
  • Robot-cell safety is governed by ISO 10218-1/-2 (and ISO/TS 15066 for collaborative operation)
  • OLP tools (DELMIA, RobotStudio, Octopuz) import CAD geometry and export vendor-specific robot programs after post-processing
