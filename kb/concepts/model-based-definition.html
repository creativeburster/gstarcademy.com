Model-Based Definition (MBD)

MBD approach: embedding PMI (Product Manufacturing Information) in 3D models to replace traditional 2D drawings.

By Gstarcademy Editorial Team Updated 2026-06-30 Editorial process

Definition & Context

Model-Based Definition (MBD) makes the annotated 3-D model — not a 2-D drawing — the authoritative product definition. GD&T, dimensions, notes, and PMI (Product and Manufacturing Information) are embedded directly on the model per ASME Y14.41 / ISO 16792.

PMI flows downstream to CAM, CMM inspection, and suppliers via formats such as 3D PDF, STEP AP242, or JT — enabling a drawingless workflow where a single source of truth drives manufacturing and quality.

Why It Matters

MBD removes the error-prone step of maintaining a separate 2-D drawing, speeds downstream reuse (CAM/CMM read PMI directly), and is increasingly mandated in aerospace and defence supply chains.

Best Practices

  • Author semantic (machine-readable) PMI, not just graphical annotations.
  • Use STEP AP242 or 3D PDF to carry PMI to suppliers and inspection.
  • Organise PMI on model views/saved states so consumers see the right info.
  • Agree the MBD standard (Y14.41) and data format with the supply chain.

Common Pitfalls

  • Adding only graphical PMI that downstream tools can't consume automatically.
  • Maintaining both a drawing and MBD, defeating the purpose.
  • Exporting to a format that strips PMI (plain STEP AP203).
  • Cluttered, unorganised annotations with no view management.
