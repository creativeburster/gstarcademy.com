Heritage Conservation (HBIM)

Professional guide to heritage conservation (hbim) — tools, workflows, software recommendations, and best practices for engineers and designers.

By Gstarcademy Editorial TeamUpdated 2026-07-28

Definition & Context

Heritage BIM (HBIM) documents and manages historic structures, capturing complex, irregular geometry via laser scanning/photogrammetry and modelling it with bespoke families and condition/deterioration data. It supports conservation planning, structural assessment, and maintenance of listed assets.

Because heritage geometry rarely fits standard parametric families, HBIM balances measured accuracy (as-found) with meaningful information (materials, condition, significance) for conservation decisions.

Why It Matters

Heritage assets demand accurate, information-rich records for conservation, repair, and compliance. HBIM preserves both geometry and the material/condition knowledge needed to care for irreplaceable structures.

Tools & Software

  • Autodesk ReCap / Leica Cyclone
  • Revit (custom families)
  • photogrammetry (RealityCapture)

Best Practices

  • Capture accurate as-found geometry via scanning/photogrammetry.
  • Model to a purpose-appropriate LOD; don't idealise irregular fabric away.
  • Attach material, condition, and significance data for conservation.
  • Coordinate structural assessment with minimal-intervention principles.

Common Pitfalls

  • Forcing irregular heritage geometry into idealised families.
  • Capturing geometry but omitting condition/significance data.
  • Over-scanning/over-modelling beyond conservation needs.
  • Interventions ignoring heritage significance and reversibility.

Standards & Interoperability

  • conservation principles (ICOMOS)
