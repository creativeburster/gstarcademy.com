Modular Construction Design

Professional guide to modular construction design — tools, workflows, software recommendations, and best practices for engineers and designers.

By Gstarcademy Editorial TeamUpdated 2026-07-28

Definition & Context

Modular/volumetric construction BIM designs buildings as factory-built 3-D modules or 2-D panels assembled on site. It requires fabrication-LOD models, rigorous interface/tolerance management between modules, transport/craning constraints, and repeatable, DfMA-driven design.

BIM coordinates module interfaces (structure, MEP connections, fire/acoustic seals) and sequences factory production and site assembly (4D), maximising standardisation and off-site completion.

Why It Matters

Modular delivery compresses schedule and improves quality by shifting work to a factory — but only when interfaces, tolerances, and transport limits are engineered precisely in the model from the outset.

Tools & Software

  • Revit (fabrication LOD)
  • Navisworks (4D)
  • DfMA-oriented workflows

Best Practices

  • Design to DfMA with standardised, repeatable modules.
  • Rigorously manage inter-module interfaces and tolerances.
  • Respect transport and craning size/weight limits.
  • Sequence factory production and site assembly with 4D.

Common Pitfalls

  • Interface/tolerance mismatches causing on-site fit failures.
  • Modules exceeding transport/craning limits.
  • Low standardisation eroding factory efficiency.
  • Design-LOD models lacking fabrication/connection detail.

Standards & Interoperability
