Casting Design for CAD

Design for casting: draft angles, fillets, parting lines, core/cavity considerations, and shrinkage allowances.

By Gstarcademy Editorial Team Updated 2026-06-30 Editorial process

Definition & Context

Casting design adapts geometry for pouring molten metal into a mould: draft angles for withdrawal, uniform wall thickness to avoid shrinkage defects, generous fillets, and allowance for machining stock on functional faces. Processes include sand, investment, and die casting, each with its own rule set.

Casting simulation (MAGMASOFT, Autodesk Netfabb/Moldflow-style tools) predicts mould filling, solidification, porosity, and hot spots so gating and risering can be optimised before tooling is cut.

Why It Matters

Casting locks in huge tooling cost. Designing for uniform solidification and clean withdrawal avoids porosity, shrink, and misruns — the defects that scrap castings and delay programs.

Best Practices

  • Maintain uniform wall thickness and blend transitions to avoid hot spots.
  • Add adequate draft and fillets for clean withdrawal and metal flow.
  • Provide machining allowance on faces that need finishing.
  • Simulate filling/solidification to place gates and risers well.

Common Pitfalls

  • Thick sections and abrupt transitions causing shrinkage porosity.
  • Insufficient draft, making parts hard to eject/withdraw.
  • Sharp internal corners creating stress and hot spots.
  • No solidification simulation, so defects appear only after tooling.
