High-Rise Building Design

Professional guide to high-rise building design — tools, workflows, software recommendations, and best practices for engineers and designers.

By Gstarcademy Editorial TeamUpdated 2026-06-30

Definition & Context

High-rise BIM coordinates the tall-building disciplines — structure (lateral systems: moment frames, shear walls, outriggers, tuned mass dampers), core, facade, and vertical MEP/transportation — where wind and seismic lateral loads dominate. Models must handle scale, repetition of typical floors, and dense service coordination.

Structural engineers analyse drift, acceleration (occupant comfort), and P-delta effects, while BIM coordinates cores, risers, and elevator zoning across dozens of storeys.

Why It Matters

Tall buildings are governed by lateral load, differential shortening, and vertical transportation efficiency. Federated BIM keeps structure, facade, and MEP coordinated across many floors, preventing costly clashes at scale.

Tools & Software

  • Revit / Tekla (structure)
  • ETABS / SAP2000 (lateral analysis)
  • Navisworks / Solibri (coordination)

Best Practices

  • Design the lateral system for drift, acceleration, and P-delta.
  • Account for differential column/core shortening over height.
  • Zone elevators and risers for efficient vertical transportation.
  • Federate and clash-coordinate dense MEP with structure/core.

Common Pitfalls

  • Checking strength but not drift/occupant-comfort acceleration.
  • Ignoring differential shortening between core and columns.
  • Under-sized vertical transportation causing lobby congestion.
  • Late MEP/structure coordination in constrained cores.

Standards & Interoperability

  • ASCE 7 (wind/seismic)
  • local building codes
