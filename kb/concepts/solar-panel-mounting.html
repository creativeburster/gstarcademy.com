Solar Panel Mounting Design

Professional guide to solar panel mounting design — tools, workflows, software recommendations, and best practices for engineers and designers.

By Gstarcademy Editorial TeamUpdated 2026-07-28

Definition & Context

Solar PV mounting design engineers the structures that hold panels — rooftop rails, ballasted flat-roof systems, and ground-mount/tracker arrays. It covers structural loads (wind, snow, seismic), tilt/orientation for yield, roof attachment/waterproofing, and electrical layout (stringing).

Tools (PVsyst for yield, Aurora/Helioscope for layout, structural analysis for mounts) size structures to code and optimise energy production against shading and site constraints.

Why It Matters

PV mounting must survive decades of wind/snow loads, protect roof integrity, and orient panels for maximum yield — structural, waterproofing, and energy considerations combined.

Tools & Software

  • PVsyst (yield)
  • Aurora / Helioscope (layout)
  • structural analysis tools

Best Practices

  • Design mounts for wind/snow/seismic loads to code.
  • Optimise tilt/orientation and spacing against shading for yield.
  • Detail roof attachment and waterproofing/flashing.
  • Coordinate structural layout with electrical stringing.

Common Pitfalls

  • Under-designed mounts failing wind uplift.
  • Roof leaks from poor attachment/flashing.
  • Sub-optimal tilt/row spacing reducing yield (self-shading).
  • Ignoring roof structural capacity for added load.

Standards & Interoperability

  • ASCE 7 loads
  • local electrical/building codes
