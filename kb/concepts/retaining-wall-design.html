Retaining Wall Design

Professional guide to retaining wall design — tools, workflows, software recommendations, and best practices for engineers and designers.

By Gstarcademy Editorial TeamUpdated 2026-06-30

Definition & Context

Retaining-wall design creates structures that hold back soil — gravity, cantilever, counterfort, MSE (mechanically stabilised earth), and soil-nailed/anchored walls — checked for external stability (sliding, overturning, bearing) and internal/structural capacity under earth and surcharge pressures.

Design uses lateral earth-pressure theory (Rankine/Coulomb), geotechnical parameters, and tools like GEO5, Wallap, or in-house spreadsheets, coordinated with drainage to relieve water pressure.

Why It Matters

Retaining walls fail expensively and dangerously if earth pressures, water, or global stability are mis-assessed. Proper geotechnical and structural checks plus drainage are essential.

Tools & Software

  • GEO5 / Wallap
  • PLAXIS (complex cases)
  • Civil 3D (grading integration)

Best Practices

  • Check external stability: sliding, overturning, and bearing.
  • Design for correct earth pressures plus surcharge and seismic where relevant.
  • Provide drainage to relieve hydrostatic pressure behind the wall.
  • Verify global slope stability for taller/anchored walls.

Common Pitfalls

  • Ignoring water pressure by omitting wall drainage.
  • Underestimating surcharge or seismic earth pressures.
  • Checking structure but missing global stability failure.
  • Wrong soil parameters from inadequate ground investigation.

Standards & Interoperability

  • AASHTO/Eurocode 7 (geotech)
