Underground Utility Design

Utility network design in civil CAD: pipe sizing, manholes, trenchless methods, and utility conflict detection.

By Gstarcademy Editorial Team Updated 2026-07-28 Editorial process

Definition & Context

Utility network design lays out and analyses water, sewer, gas, electrical, and telecom systems, both above and below ground. Civil/BIM tools model gravity and pressure networks (invert levels, slopes, pressures), detect conflicts with other utilities and structures, and export data for GIS and asset management.

3-D utility modelling supports clash detection against structures and other services, and feeds construction/as-built records into utility GIS systems.

Why It Matters

Coordinated utility design prevents the costly clashes and rework that plague congested corridors, and produces reliable records for operation and maintenance.

Best Practices

  • Model utilities in 3-D and clash-check against structures and each other.
  • Maintain correct inverts/slopes for gravity systems and pressures for mains.
  • Coordinate horizontal/vertical separation per code between services.
  • Capture accurate as-builts for utility GIS/asset management.

Common Pitfalls

  • 2-D-only layouts hiding vertical conflicts.
  • Wrong inverts/slopes causing gravity systems not to drain.
  • Insufficient separation between services violating code.
  • Poor as-built records degrading downstream GIS.
