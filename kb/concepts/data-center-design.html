Data Center Design in BIM

Professional guide to data center design in bim — tools, workflows, software recommendations, and best practices for engineers and designers.

By Gstarcademy Editorial TeamUpdated 2026-06-30

Definition & Context

Data-center design engineers highly-available facilities for IT load: power (redundant UPS, generators, PDUs), cooling (CRAC/CRAH, hot/cold aisle containment, liquid cooling), space (racks, containment), and resilience classified by the Uptime Institute Tier system. Power and cooling density (kW/rack) drive the design.

BIM/MEP coordinates dense power and cooling infrastructure, and computational fluid dynamics (CFD) analyses airflow and hotspots; PUE (Power Usage Effectiveness) measures efficiency.

Why It Matters

Data centers are power- and cooling-intensive, availability-critical facilities. Redundancy, thermal management, and efficiency (PUE) must be engineered precisely — downtime and inefficiency are extremely costly.

Tools & Software

  • Revit MEP
  • 6SigmaDCX / CFD (airflow)
  • electrical design tools

Best Practices

  • Design power/cooling redundancy to the target Uptime Tier.
  • Manage airflow with containment; verify with CFD for hotspots.
  • Design for the required rack power density and future growth.
  • Optimise efficiency (PUE) without compromising resilience.

Common Pitfalls

  • Hotspots from poor airflow/containment design.
  • Redundancy not matching the target availability tier.
  • Under-provisioned power/cooling for actual rack density.
  • Chasing low PUE at the expense of resilience.

Standards & Interoperability

  • Uptime Tier
  • ASHRAE TC 9.9 / TIA-942
