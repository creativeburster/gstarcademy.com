Sheet Metal K-Factor and Bend Allowance

The mathematical ratio that locates the neutral axis during sheet metal bending, essential for calculating exact flat pattern cut lengths.

By Gstarcademy Editorial Team Updated 2026-09-30 Editorial process

1. Definition & Engineering Significance

When metal bends, the outer fibers stretch in tension while the inner fibers compress. The neutral axis experiences zero length change. The K-factor (ratio of neutral axis depth to total sheet thickness) dictates the precise unfolded blank dimensions required before press-brake stamping.

2. Key Technical Characteristics & Rules

  • Typical K-Factor Range: Between 0.33 for sharp air bending up to 0.50 for large radius bending or soft aluminum.
  • Bend Deduction (BD): The amount subtracted from the sum of external flange lengths to determine total unfolded blank length.
  • Material Yield Influence: High tensile steel shifts the neutral axis closer to the inner radius compared to mild carbon steel.

3. Neutral Axis Physics & Flat Blank Formulas

When sheet metal bends, compressive stresses deform the inner radius while tensile stresses stretch the outer radius:

  • K-Factor Definition: The ratio of the neutral axis position (t) to total material thickness (T): K = t / T. Typical air-bending K-factors range from 0.33 to 0.45.
  • Bend Allowance (BA) Formula: BA = π * (R + K * T) * (θ / 180), calculating the arc length along the unstretched neutral layer.
  • Bend Deduction (BD): The amount subtracted from the sum of the flange lengths to calculate the precise flat blank cutting pattern.

4. Press Brake Tooling & Empirical Calibration

Using CAD default K-factors (such as 0.50) without calibration results in flat blanks that are consistently 1-2mm too long when bent on shop-floor press brakes. Quality managers must run physical bend coupon tests for each specific material gauge, V-die opening width, and punch radius to establish verified bend tables.
