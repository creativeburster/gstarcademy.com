Energy Modeling from BIM

Using BIM models for energy simulation: gbXML export, thermal zones, HVAC modeling, and daylighting analysis.

By Gstarcademy Editorial Team Updated 2026-07-28 Editorial process

Definition & Context

Building energy modelling (BEM) simulates a building's thermal loads and annual energy use from a geometric and material model. Engines include the DOE's EnergyPlus, IES VE, DesignBuilder, and Autodesk Insight. Geometry is exchanged via gbXML or IFC.

Analysis ranges from early-stage conceptual massing studies (orientation, glazing ratio, shading) to detailed compliance modelling for codes such as ASHRAE 90.1, and drives decisions on envelope, HVAC sizing, and renewable integration toward net-zero targets.

Why It Matters

Energy modelling lets teams cut operational carbon and running costs when changes are cheapest — in early design — and is required for green-building certification and many building codes.

Best Practices

  • Run conceptual energy studies early when massing and orientation can still change.
  • Build a clean analytical model (rooms/spaces, correct adjacencies) — messy geometry breaks BEM.
  • Use realistic schedules, internal gains, and weather files for the actual location.
  • Calibrate detailed models against metered data where available.

Common Pitfalls

  • Exporting messy architectural geometry that the energy engine can't interpret.
  • Default schedules and gains that don't match real building use.
  • Wrong or generic weather file for the site.
  • Treating early-stage results as precise compliance numbers.
