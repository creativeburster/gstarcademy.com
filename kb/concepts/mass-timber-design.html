Mass Timber Design in BIM

Professional guide to mass timber design in bim — tools, workflows, software recommendations, and best practices for engineers and designers.

By Gstarcademy Editorial TeamUpdated 2026-07-28

Definition & Context

Mass-timber design uses engineered wood — CLT (cross-laminated timber), glulam, LVL, and DLT — for structural floors, walls, and frames as a low-carbon alternative to steel/concrete. Design addresses connections, vibration, fire (charring rate), acoustics, and moisture, with tight fabrication tolerances for prefab panels.

BIM models integrate timber-specific detailing and connections; fabrication data drives CNC cutting of panels. Codes now permit tall timber buildings under specific fire/structural provisions.

Why It Matters

Mass timber offers speed, low embodied carbon, and prefabrication benefits, but demands careful attention to fire charring, vibration, moisture, and connection detailing that differ fundamentally from steel/concrete.

Tools & Software

  • Revit + timber add-ins
  • Cadwork / hsbCAD (timber CNC)
  • structural analysis (RFEM/Dlubal)

Best Practices

  • Design connections and detailing specific to CLT/glulam behaviour.
  • Check floor vibration and acoustic separation for timber floors.
  • Design fire resistance via charring rate and encapsulation.
  • Protect timber from moisture during construction and in service.

Common Pitfalls

  • Applying steel/concrete connection logic to timber.
  • Ignoring floor vibration/acoustic performance.
  • Underestimating fire charring or moisture exposure.
  • Loose tolerances defeating prefab timber's precision benefit.

Standards & Interoperability

  • Eurocode 5 / NDS (timber)
  • tall-timber code provisions
