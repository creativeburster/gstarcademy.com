Aircraft Structural Design

Professional guide to aircraft structural design — tools, workflows, software recommendations, and best practices for engineers and designers.

By Gstarcademy Editorial TeamUpdated 2026-07-28

Definition & Context

Aircraft structural design creates the load-bearing airframe — wings, fuselage, empennage, and joints — balancing minimum weight against strength, stiffness, fatigue, and damage tolerance. Structures are largely thin-walled semi-monocoque (skins, stringers, frames, spars) in aluminium alloys and increasingly carbon-fibre composites.

The workflow couples parametric CAD with FEA (static, buckling, and fatigue/damage-tolerance) and certification to airworthiness requirements. Loads come from flight and ground load cases, gust and manoeuvre envelopes, and pressurisation cycles.

Why It Matters

Airframe weight directly drives fuel burn and payload, while structural integrity is life-critical and certification-gated. Every gram and every fatigue detail matters, making rigorous analysis and traceability essential.

Tools & Software

  • CATIA (aerospace standard)
  • Siemens NX
  • ANSYS / MSC Nastran / Abaqus (FEA)
  • HyperWorks (optimisation)

Best Practices

  • Design to explicit load cases and include fatigue/damage-tolerance from the start.
  • Exploit composites' directional properties with proper laminate/ply design.
  • Maintain full traceability of analysis and material allowables for certification.
  • Optimise for weight but verify buckling and joint strength, not just stress.

Common Pitfalls

  • Static strength checks only, ignoring fatigue and damage tolerance.
  • Treating composites as isotropic like metals.
  • Missing buckling of thin-walled panels under compression.
  • Poor traceability that fails certification audits.

Standards & Interoperability

  • FAR/CS-25 airworthiness
  • MMPDS material allowables
