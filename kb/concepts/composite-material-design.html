Composite Material Design

Professional guide to composite material design in CAD/BIM engineering workflows.

By Gstarcademy Editorial TeamUpdated 2026-06-30

Definition & Context

Composite design engineers fibre-reinforced parts (carbon/glass in epoxy) where the laminate — fibre orientation, ply stacking sequence, and thickness — is designed alongside the shape. Classical Lamination Theory and composite FEA predict stiffness, strength (failure criteria like Tsai-Wu), and failure modes such as delamination.

Tools (ANSYS Composite PrepPost, Abaqus, Fibersim, HyperWorks) design plies, generate flat patterns for layup, and analyse ply-by-ply; manufacturing (hand layup, prepreg/autoclave, RTM, AFP) constrains the design.

Why It Matters

Composites are designed, not just selected — the laminate is part of the design. Exploiting directional properties while managing delamination and manufacturability is what makes composites efficient.

Tools & Software

  • ANSYS Composite PrepPost
  • Abaqus
  • Siemens Fibersim
  • HyperWorks

Best Practices

  • Design ply orientation/stacking for the load paths (tailor directionality).
  • Use composite failure criteria and check interlaminar/delamination.
  • Design for the manufacturing method (drape, AFP, RTM) and layup.
  • Manage free edges, ply drops, and joints carefully.

Common Pitfalls

  • Treating composites as isotropic 'black metal'.
  • Ignoring delamination/interlaminar failure.
  • Laminates that can't be draped/manufactured.
  • Poor ply-drop/joint detailing causing stress concentrations.
