Weld Symbols & Joint Design

AWS A2.4 weld symbols in CAD drawings: joint types, groove geometry, weld sizing, and annotation standards.

By Gstarcademy Editorial Team Updated 2026-06-30 Editorial process

Definition & Context

Weld symbols are the standardised notation (per AWS A2.4 in the US, ISO 2553 internationally) that specify weld type, size, length, location, and process on drawings. The symbol's reference line, arrow, weld-type symbol (fillet, groove, plug), and dimensions communicate exactly what to weld and how.

In CAD/BIM, weldment environments (SOLIDWORKS Weldments, Inventor Frame Generator, Tekla) model weld beads and generate weld symbols and cut lists, coordinating with structural connection design.

Why It Matters

Weld symbols precisely communicate joint requirements to fabricators. Misread or wrong symbols cause under/over-welding — affecting strength, cost, and distortion — so correct notation is safety-critical.

Best Practices

  • Use the governing standard (AWS A2.4 or ISO 2553) consistently.
  • Place weld-type symbols on the correct side of the reference line.
  • Specify size, length, and pitch (intermittent welds) explicitly.
  • Coordinate weld callouts with connection design and access.

Common Pitfalls

  • Mixing AWS and ISO conventions (arrow/other-side differ).
  • Omitting weld size/length, leaving it to the fabricator to guess.
  • Over-specifying weld size, adding cost and distortion.
  • Symbols placed where the joint can't actually be welded.
