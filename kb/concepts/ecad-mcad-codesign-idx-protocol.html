ECAD-MCAD CoDesign & the IDX Exchange Protocol

The standardized protocol facilitating incremental collaborative changes between electronic PCB layout designers and mechanical enclosure engineers.

By GstarCAD Academy Engineering Editorial Board (Suzhou Gstarsoft Co., Ltd.) Reviewed by Chen Haoran, M.Sc., GDTP-S (Principal Mechanical CAD & GD&T Metrology Lead) Last reviewed 2026-09-30 Editorial Process & Testbed

1. Definition & Engineering Significance

Historically, exchanging PCB data required monolithic IDF or STEP file exports, losing change histories and component metadata. The Incremental Design Exchange (IDX) protocol tracks changes incrementally (e.g., 'capacitor C12 moved 2mm left, mounting hole enlarged to M3'), enabling collaborative accept/reject workflows.

2. Key Technical Characteristics & Rules

  • Keepout Zones & 3D Component Envelopes: Mechanical engineers define allowable component height zones; ECAD software automatically checks clearance.
  • Connector Alignment Validation: Verifies that PCB USB ports, LEDs, and pushbuttons align perfectly with injection-molded enclosure cutouts.
  • Thermal & Copper Synchronization: Transfers copper layer stackups and power dissipation hot-spots directly into FEA/CFD thermal analysis solvers.

3. Incremental Collaboration via ProStep IDX

The ProStep EDMD (ECAD/MCAD Data Exchange) XML schema standardizes incremental collaborative workflows between electrical PCB tools (Altium, Cadence) and mechanical CAD systems (SolidWorks, Creo, NX):

  • Baseline & Incremental Changes: Instead of re-importing massive 3D models upon every design tweak, IDX communicates only moved component centroids, modified board outlines, and altered keep-out zones.
  • Accept / Reject Messaging: Electrical and mechanical engineers review proposed component shifts interactively, approving or rejecting changes with embedded engineering notes.
  • 3D Component Heights: Maps precise component bounding heights to prevent collisions with metal chassis covers and thermal heatsinks.

4. Coordinate Origin Alignment & Keep-Out Zones

A primary failure point in ECAD-MCAD collaboration is misaligned coordinate origins. Teams must establish a shared physical mounting hole or board corner as the master 0,0,0 reference. Mechanical designers must explicitly define Z-direction keep-out volumes around connectors to prevent electrical trace routing in high-stress assembly zones.

Enterprise Production Implementation & GstarCAD 2027 Workflow

In industrial engineering production, mastering ECAD-MCAD CoDesign & the IDX Exchange Protocol is essential for maintaining strict drawing database hygiene, geometric precision, and cross-disciplinary interoperability across DWG, BIM (IFC 4.3), and mechanical (STEP AP242 / ASME Y14.5) deliverables. When implementing this concept within GstarCAD 2027 Professional, GstarCAD Mechanical, or heterogeneous multi-CAD environments, engineering teams should adhere to the following standardized verification protocol:

  • Database Serialization & Binary Fidelity: Ensure all entities and symbol table records associated with ECAD-MCAD CoDesign & the IDX Exchange Protocol are authored on standardized ISO 13567 layers rather than Layer 0 or Defpoints, and verify lossless round-trip persistence in native DWG (AC1032) format.
  • System Variable & Template Standardization: Embed pre-validated dimension styles, annotative scale lists (SCALELISTEDIT), and plotting configurations directly into your corporate .dwt seed templates so drafters never configure parameters manually from scratch.
  • Multi-Core Performance & QA Auditing: Prior to issuing construction or fabrication transmittals (ETRANSMIT), execute automated geometry audits (OVERKILL to eliminate duplicate overlapping segments, AUDIT to verify object handles, and -PURGE to strip orphaned registered applications).
