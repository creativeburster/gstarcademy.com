Drawings & Detailing

SOLIDWORKS drawing environment for creating production drawings with views, annotations, BOM, and GD&T.

By Gstarcademy Editorial Team Updated 2026-07-28 Editorial process

Definition & Context

The SOLIDWORKS drawing environment produces 2D production drawings from 3D models: drawing views (standard, section, detail, broken-out, auxiliary), dimensions and annotations, GD&T, bill of materials, and revision tables — all associative to the model, so views update when the part or assembly changes.

Drawings use sheet formats and templates for title blocks and standards; model dimensions can be imported or added as reference. DimXpert/MBD data can drive annotations, and eDrawings shares the result for review.

Why It Matters

Associative drawings mean documentation tracks the model automatically, eliminating the drift between a drawing and the part it represents.

Standardized templates plus a driven BOM and GD&T are what make drawings manufacturable and compliant, not just pictures.

Best Practices

  • Build title blocks and standards into sheet formats and templates
  • Keep views associative; avoid detached or edited geometry that won't update
  • Use GD&T and a driven BOM rather than manually typed notes and quantities
  • Set the drafting standard (ISO/ANSI) and dimension styles at the template level

Common Pitfalls

  • Manually editing dimensions or BOM values that then drift from the model
  • Detailing on a model that isn't finalized, forcing rework
  • Inconsistent sheet formats/standards across a drawing set
