Passive House Design in BIM

BIM workflows for Passive House certification: thermal bridging analysis, PHPP integration, and envelope optimization.

By GstarCAD Academy Engineering Editorial Board (Suzhou Gstarsoft Co., Ltd.) Reviewed by GstarCAD Academy Editorial Team Last reviewed 2026-08-16 Editorial Process

Definition & Context

Passive House (Passivhaus) is a rigorous low-energy building standard achieving comfort with minimal heating/cooling via a super-insulated, airtight envelope, thermal-bridge-free detailing, high-performance glazing, and heat-recovery ventilation (MVHR). Compliance is verified with the PHPP energy model against strict targets (heating demand ≤15 kWh/m²·yr, airtightness ≤0.6 ACH50).

BIM supports Passive House through accurate envelope modelling, thermal-bridge analysis, and coordination of airtightness layers and ventilation.

Why It Matters

Passive House delivers ~80–90% heating-energy reduction and excellent comfort. Meeting its strict, verified targets requires careful envelope modelling and detailing that BIM and PHPP make tractable.

Best Practices

  • Design a continuous insulation and airtightness layer; detail out thermal bridges.
  • Model and verify with PHPP against the standard's targets.
  • Specify high-performance glazing and MVHR sized correctly.
  • Coordinate the airtight layer across all trades in the model.

Common Pitfalls

  • Discontinuous insulation/airtightness lines that leak performance.
  • Unaddressed thermal bridges undermining the envelope.
  • Skipping PHPP verification and missing targets.
  • MVHR poorly commissioned, hurting comfort and efficiency.

Horizontal curve radius triggers AASHTO violation flagsAdjust minimum transition spiral length or increase curve radius to meet minimum design speed superelevation criteria.
