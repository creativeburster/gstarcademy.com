Technical Drawings General Principles (ISO 128)

The international standard defining line types, line widths, cutting planes, views, and section representations on mechanical and technical drawings.

By GstarCAD Academy Engineering Editorial Board (Suzhou Gstarsoft Co., Ltd.) Reviewed by Chen Haoran, M.Sc., GDTP-S (Principal Mechanical CAD & GD&T Metrology Lead) Last reviewed 2026-09-30 Editorial Process & Testbed

1. Definition & Engineering Significance

ISO 128 establishes universal clarity on engineering documentation. It dictates line weight hierarchies (0.5mm continuous wide for visible edges, 0.25mm dashed for hidden details, 0.25mm chain-thin for centerlines), eliminating ambiguity across international supply chains.

2. Key Technical Characteristics & Rules

  • Line Grouping: Standardized 1
  • Section Hatching: 45-degree equidistant thin lines differentiate solid cuts from voids; alternating angles distinguish adjacent assembled components.
  • Replaced Legacy National Codes: Unifies DIN 6, BS 308, and ANSI standards into an ISO-compliant baseline.

3. Line Types, Weights & Engineering Application

ISO 128 defines standardized graphical representations across all mechanical and civil engineering drawings:

  • Continuous Thick Lines (Type 01.2): Visible outlines and structural edges (typically 0.50mm or 0.70mm).
  • Dashed Narrow Lines (Type 02.1): Hidden contours and concealed features (typically 0.25mm or 0.35mm).
  • Long-Dashed Dotted Narrow Lines (Type 04.1): Axes of symmetry, pitch circles, and movement trajectories.
  • Continuous Narrow with Zigzags (Type 01.1): Interrupted views and long-break boundaries.

4. Plot Style Governance & Sheet Uniformity

Maintain enterprise CAD standards by linking ISO 128 line weights directly to drawing layer conventions through standardized CTB or STB plot style tables. Avoid overriding line weights on individual entity properties, which complicates multi-sheet drafting updates and creates unreadable micro-lineweights on PDF exports.
