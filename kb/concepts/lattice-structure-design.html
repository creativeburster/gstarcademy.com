Lattice Structure Design

Professional guide to lattice structure design in CAD/BIM engineering workflows.

By Gstarcademy Editorial TeamUpdated 2026-06-30

Definition & Context

Lattice structure design creates repeating cellular geometry — strut-based (BCC, FCC, octet) or surface-based (TPMS: gyroid, Schwarz) — to achieve tailored stiffness, energy absorption, weight, and heat/flow properties. Lattices are computationally generated and graded, and are enabled by additive manufacturing.

Tools like nTopology generate and conform lattices to a volume with graded density, and homogenised/FEA methods predict effective properties for structural, thermal, or biomedical (bone in-growth) uses.

Why It Matters

Lattices give lightweight, tunable performance — impact absorption, lightweighting, implants — unachievable with solid material, and are a signature capability of AM design.

Tools & Software

  • nTopology
  • Autodesk Within/Fusion
  • FEA (homogenisation)

Best Practices

  • Choose cell type (strut vs TPMS) for the target property.
  • Grade density to place material where loads/functions require.
  • Ensure minimum strut/wall size is printable and self-draining.
  • Predict effective properties with homogenisation/FEA.

Common Pitfalls

  • Lattices below printable feature size or trapping powder.
  • Uniform lattices where grading would perform better.
  • Assuming lattice properties without analysis.
  • Stress concentrations at lattice-to-solid transitions.

Standards & Interoperability
