Contact Analysis in FEA

Contact modeling in structural FEA: contact types, friction, penetration tolerance, and solver convergence for assemblies.

By Gstarcademy Editorial Team Updated 2026-07-28 Editorial process

Definition & Context

Contact analysis models the interaction between touching bodies that can separate, slide, and transmit compressive normal and frictional tangential forces. It is inherently nonlinear because the contact status (open/closed/sliding) changes during loading, so the stiffness matrix is updated iteratively.

Solvers enforce non-penetration with the penalty method (a contact stiffness), Lagrange multipliers (exact, no penetration but extra DOF), or the augmented Lagrangian hybrid. Formulations include bonded, no-separation, frictionless, and frictional (Coulomb μ). Convergence is often the hardest part of a structural model.

Why It Matters

Real assemblies transmit load through bolted, pressed, and bearing contacts. Correct contact modelling governs load paths, stress concentrations, and joint stiffness — bonding everything together gives an unrealistically stiff, wrong answer.

Best Practices

  • Start with the simplest valid contact (bonded) then add frictional contact only where sliding matters.
  • Use augmented Lagrangian for accuracy when penetration must be minimal.
  • Refine the mesh on contacting faces so pressure distribution is captured.
  • Add small stabilising features (weak springs, ramped loads) to aid convergence of free bodies.
  • Verify contact pressure and status plots match physical expectation.

Common Pitfalls

  • Bonding all interfaces for convenience, over-stiffening the assembly.
  • Excessive penalty stiffness causing solver oscillation, or too little causing penetration.
  • Rigid-body motion from a part held only by not-yet-closed contacts.
  • Coarse mesh on contact faces smearing the true pressure peak.
