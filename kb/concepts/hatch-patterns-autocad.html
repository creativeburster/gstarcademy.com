Hatch Patterns

AutoCAD hatching system for filling boundaries with patterns, solid colors, or gradients indicating materials in sections.

By Gstarcademy Editorial Team Updated 2026-07-28 Editorial process

Definition & Context

Hatching fills a bounded area with a pattern, solid color, or gradient via the HATCH command. AutoCAD ships ANSI/ISO material patterns (.pat files), and you can load custom patterns. Hatches can be associative — updating when their boundary changes — and use annotative scaling.

Boundaries are found by picking an internal point or selecting objects, with a gap tolerance for nearly-closed areas. Pattern scale, angle, origin, and layer all affect appearance, and the “separate hatches” option controls whether one command yields one or many objects.

Why It Matters

Hatching communicates materials and section cuts in construction and mechanical drawings; correct pattern, scale, and layering are part of drawing-standard compliance, not decoration.

Associative hatches save rework — edit the boundary and the fill follows — but only while the boundary stays valid.

Best Practices

  • Place hatches on a dedicated thin/low-priority layer and keep them associative
  • Use annotative hatch scale so patterns read correctly at the plotted scale
  • Set a small gap tolerance for nearly-closed boundaries instead of drawing closing lines
  • Pick boundaries carefully on large areas to avoid slow boundary evaluation

Common Pitfalls

  • Very dense pattern scale on large areas — huge object counts and slow drawings/plots
  • Non-associative hatches that don't follow boundary edits
  • Hatching across an open boundary, producing an incorrect or failed fill
