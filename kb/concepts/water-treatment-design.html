Water Treatment Plant Design

Professional guide to water treatment plant design — tools, workflows, software recommendations, and best practices for engineers and designers.

By Gstarcademy Editorial TeamUpdated 2026-07-28

Definition & Context

Water/wastewater treatment plant design lays out and sizes process units — screening, sedimentation, filtration, biological reactors, disinfection — and the pipework, pumps, and controls connecting them. It combines process engineering (hydraulic profile, loading rates) with 3-D plant modelling.

BIM/plant tools (Revit, Plant 3D, Bentley OpenPlant) model tanks, pipes, and equipment, coordinate MEP/electrical, and generate P&IDs, isometrics, and quantities.

Why It Matters

Treatment plants protect public health and the environment. Correct process sizing, hydraulic profiles, and 3-D coordination of complex pipework and equipment ensure reliable, maintainable operation.

Tools & Software

  • AutoCAD Plant 3D
  • Revit
  • Bentley OpenPlant
  • process/hydraulic tools

Best Practices

  • Size process units to loading and regulatory effluent standards.
  • Develop the plant hydraulic profile so flow is gravity-consistent.
  • Model pipework/equipment in 3-D and coordinate access/maintenance.
  • Generate P&IDs, isometrics, and quantities from the model.

Common Pitfalls

  • Under-sized units failing effluent-quality requirements.
  • Hydraulic-profile errors preventing gravity flow.
  • Pipework clashes and poor maintenance access.
  • P&IDs inconsistent with the 3-D model.

Standards & Interoperability

  • EPA/local discharge standards
