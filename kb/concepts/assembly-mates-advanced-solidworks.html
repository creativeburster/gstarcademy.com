Advanced Mates & Motion

SOLIDWORKS advanced mate types: gear, rack-pinion, cam, slot, path, and limit mates for mechanical motion simulation.

By Gstarcademy Editorial Team Updated 2026-06-30 Editorial process

Definition & Context

Beyond standard mates (Coincident, Parallel, Perpendicular, Tangent, Concentric, Distance, Angle), SOLIDWORKS provides advanced mates that define mechanical relationships: Gear (ratio-linked rotation), Rack-Pinion (linear-to-rotational), Cam (follower traces cam profile), Slot (pin-in-slot travel), Path (component follows 3D curve), Linear/Linear Coupler (proportional movement), and Limit Mates (distance/angle min-max).

Mechanical mates enable motion simulation without Motion Study: drag a gear and its mating gear counter-rotates at the defined ratio. Limit mates define travel stops — a slider that can move 0-150mm. Width mate centers a component between two parallel faces. Symmetry mate mirrors position across a reference plane.

Mate references (published on part features) auto-apply mates during insertion: drop a bolt into a hole and coincident + concentric mates apply automatically. SmartMates drag-and-drop creates mates by proximity detection.

Why It Matters

Mastery of Advanced Mates & Motion directly impacts productivity, output quality, and cross-discipline collaboration in SOLIDWORKS projects. Engineers and designers who command this feature reduce rework cycles, meet tighter deadlines with fewer errors, and produce deliverables that downstream consumers (fabricators, analysts, facility managers) can use without manual data reconstruction.

In competitive workflows where multiple stakeholders depend on model data quality, proficiency with Advanced Mates & Motion distinguishes experienced practitioners who deliver right-first-time results from novice users who generate downstream coordination issues.

Best Practices

  • Use Gear mates with correct tooth ratios for mechanism visualization — more accurate than rotating manually for interference checks
  • Apply Limit mates to all linear actuators and hinges to prevent physically impossible travel in mechanism studies
  • Publish Mate References on standard parts (fasteners, bearings, connectors) to enable one-click assembly insertion
  • Use Width mates for components that must stay centered (drawer slides, linear bearings) regardless of housing dimension changes
  • Combine Path mate with Tangent mate for cam-follower mechanisms that maintain surface contact along arbitrary profiles

Common Pitfalls

  • Gear mates only link rotation — they don't enforce center-distance or mesh interference; validate with Contact in Motion Analysis
  • Cam mates with sharp profile corners cause solver instability — add fillets to cam profiles for smooth follower contact
  • Over-mating assemblies causes over-defined warnings; use minimum mates to fully position components (6 DOF reduced to 0)
  • SmartMates can create unintended mate types — always verify the mate type in the PropertyManager after quick-drag insertion
