LOD (Level of Development)

BIM Level of Development framework (LOD 100-500) defining model detail and reliability at each project stage.

By Gstarcademy Editorial Team Updated 2026-06-30 Editorial process

Definition & Context

Level of Development (LOD) specifies how reliable and detailed a BIM element is at a given stage. The BIMForum/AIA scale runs LOD 100 (conceptual/symbolic), 200 (approximate geometry), 300 (precise geometry, modelled), 350 (interfaces with other systems), 400 (fabrication-ready), to 500 (verified as-built).

LOD combines Level of Detail (graphical richness) and Level of Information (attached data). ISO 19650 uses the related concept of Level of Information Need (LOIN) to avoid over-modelling by specifying exactly what geometry and data each purpose requires.

Why It Matters

LOD sets clear expectations about what a model can be relied upon for, preventing both under-modelling (unusable) and over-modelling (wasted effort) and forming a contractual basis for deliverables.

Best Practices

  • Specify required LOD per element and per stage in the BEP — not a blanket LOD for the whole model.
  • Distinguish Level of Detail from Level of Information — a box with rich data can be more useful than a detailed dumb solid.
  • Use LOIN (ISO 19650) to model only what each use case actually needs.
  • Verify LOD 500 against site reality before declaring as-built.

Common Pitfalls

  • Applying one LOD to an entire model regardless of element purpose.
  • Modelling to LOD 400 everywhere and wasting effort on non-critical elements.
  • Confusing graphical detail with data completeness.
  • Assuming a high LOD implies the data is verified/as-built.
