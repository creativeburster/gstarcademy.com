FEA Shear Locking & Hourglassing: Numerical Remedies in Solid Elements

Technical guide to shear locking in fully integrated linear elements and hourglass zero-energy modes in reduced integration elements in structural FEA.

By Gstarcademy Technical Review Board Updated 2026-10-16 Editorial process

Strict adherence to international ISO, ASME, and buildingSMART technical standards.
🛡️ Peer-Reviewed Methodology:

1. Definition & Mathematical Engineering Foundations

In structural Finite Element Analysis (FEA), numerical element formulation errors can invalidate stress and deflection results. The two most notorious pathological numerical issues in solid and shell elements are Shear Locking (which plagues fully integrated first-order linear elements) and Hourglassing (which plagues reduced-integration elements). Understanding these numerical phenomena is essential for ensuring simulation accuracy across commercial solvers like ANSYS, Abaqus, and Nastran.

2. Technical Architecture & Key Formulations

  • Shear Locking Mechanism: First-order 4-node quads and 8-node hex bricks cannot model pure curved bending. Instead, the mathematical shape functions introduce artificial parasitic shear strains, making the element orders of magnitude artificially stiffer than physical reality.
  • Remedy 1: Quadratic Elements: Switching to second-order 20-node hexahedral (SOLID186) or 10-node tetrahedral (SOLID187) elements completely eliminates shear locking because parabolic shape functions model pure bending without shear.
  • Hourglassing (Zero-Energy Modes): Reduced-integration elements (e.g., 1-point integration in explicit dynamics) eliminate shear locking and calculate 4x faster, but develop unphysical zigzag mesh deformation modes that produce zero strain and zero stress.
  • Remedy 2: Hourglass Control Viscosity: Solvers inject artificial stiffness or viscous damping (Flanagan-Belytschko or perturbation hourglass control) to suppress zigzag modes while maintaining high computational throughput.

3. Industry Failure Modes & Risk Mitigation

A classic simulation trap occurs when an analyst models a thin cantilever beam using a single layer of standard linear 8-node brick elements. Due to shear locking, the FEA model predicts only 10% of the true physical deflection, leading engineers to believe a dangerously flexible structure is adequately rigid.

4. Production Governance & Metrological Standards

FEA best practices require auditing total hourglass energy ($ALLAE$) in explicit solvers: hourglass energy must remain below 5% of internal strain energy ($ALLIE$) to guarantee physically meaningful crash and impact simulations.
