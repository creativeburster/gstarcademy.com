Skeleton Modeling

The blueprint of complexity: Using master components to drive assembly-wide synchronization and interface control.

Centralized Control

In massive assemblies (like a car engine or aircraft wing), you cannot afford for parts to be modeled in isolation. Skeleton Modeling in Creo provides a central 'geometry container' that defines the overall space, key axes, and mating surfaces. Every part in the assembly 'looks' at the Skeleton for its instructions.

Skeleton Advantages

  • Parallel Engineering: Multiple designers can work on individual parts simultaneously because the interface (defined in the skeleton) is fixed.
  • Rapid Iteration: Change a single surface in the Skeleton, and every part touching that surface updates automatically.
  • Bill of Materials (BOM) Hygiene: Skeletons are automatically excluded from mass calculations and BOM lists.
  • Motion Control: Use Skeletons to define the kinematic range of a mechanism without adding the weight of solid parts.

The 'Copy Geometry' Workflow

Data is moved from the Skeleton to individual parts using 'Copy Geometry' or 'Publish Geometry' features. This creates a one-way, stable link that prevents circular references while maintaining full associativity.
