Multi-Body Part Modeling

Multi-body design techniques: creating multiple solid bodies in one part file for complex components and weldments.

By Gstarcademy Editorial Team Updated 2026-06-30 Editorial process

Definition & Context

Multi-body modelling keeps several solid bodies within a single part file, letting designers build interacting geometry (e.g. master-model surfaces split into components) before splitting them into separate parts. Bodies can be combined, split, and referenced without the overhead of an assembly.

It underpins top-down design and master-model techniques: a controlling body or surface defines shared interfaces, and Boolean/split operations derive the individual components that inherit that intent.

Why It Matters

Multi-body modelling is ideal for designing tightly-coupled geometry (housings that mate, split lines, weldments) in one context, ensuring parts fit by construction rather than by matching numbers across files.

Best Practices

  • Use a master body/surface to drive shared interfaces, then split to parts.
  • Name and organise bodies clearly for downstream selection.
  • Keep Boolean history clean so edits regenerate predictably.
  • Convert to an assembly when parts need independent lifecycle/config.

Common Pitfalls

  • Cramming an entire assembly into one part file and losing manageability.
  • Unnamed bodies that are impossible to select reliably.
  • Fragile Boolean chains that fail on edit.
  • Not transitioning to an assembly when BOM/config is needed.
