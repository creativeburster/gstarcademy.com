How to Setup Local Mesh Refinement & Verify Mesh Convergence in ANSYS Mechanical

Master finite element mesh independence studies in ANSYS Mechanical: apply local sizing, inflation layers, quadratic tetrahedral/hexahedral elements, and convergence criteria while avoiding stress singularities.

Author: Gstarcademy Engineering Editorial Board Updated: 2026-10-12

⚡ Quick Answer / Key Takeaway

Use second-order (Quadratic) elements, apply local Body/Face/Sphere of Influence Sizing at high-gradient fillets, insert a Convergence object under your Equivalent (von Mises) Stress result with an allowable change of 2%–5%, or perform a 3-level manual h-refinement study (h, h/2, h/4).

Step-by-Step Instructions & Commands

Step 1: Enforce Quadratic Elements & Local Feature Sizing

Never refine the entire global assembly uniformly—that explodes solve time by $O(N^3)$. In ANSYS Mechanical **Mesh** details, set **Element Order** to `Quadratic` (10-node tetrahedrals or 20-node hexahedrals to prevent shear locking). Insert local **Sizing** controls on critical load-bearing fillets, ensuring at least 3–4 elements span across the fillet arc and through thin wall thicknesses.

Step 2: Configure Adaptive Convergence or 3-Point h-Refinement

Right-click your **Equivalent (von Mises) Stress** result (scoped strictly to the physical fillet face, never the entire body) and insert a **Convergence** tool. Set **Allowable Change** to `3%` and max refinement loops to `3` under Analysis Settings. Alternatively, parameterize local element size $h$ in Workbench (`4mm`, `2mm`, `1mm`) and plot stress vs degrees of freedom to confirm asymptotic flattening.

Step 3: Diagnose & Isolate Artificial Stress Singularities

If peak stress climbs endlessly (+30% on every refinement pass) without leveling off, the peak is located at a **Mathematical Stress Singularity**—such as a sharp 0mm internal re-entrant corner, a point load, or the edge of a rigid Fixed Support. Scope your stress probe away from the idealized boundary constraint (per Saint-Venant's Principle) or model the true physical fillet radius.

Common Issues & Troubleshooting Guide

Common Failure / Problem Root Cause & Recommended Solution
Adaptive convergence fails because geometry has sweeping or hex meshing ANSYS automated adaptive h-refinement only supports tetrahedral meshes. For hex-dominant or swept bodies, use parameterized Body of Influence (BOI) sizing in the Workbench Parameter Set.
Unaveraged nodal stress plot shows colorful checkerboard bands Compare 'Unaveraged' vs 'Averaged' contour results. If the discrepancy at your critical fillet exceeds 5%–10%, the local mesh is still too coarse to resolve the strain gradient.

Cross-CAD Compatibility & Alternatives

Before meshing in ANSYS, Abaqus, or COMSOL, defeature tiny cosmetic logos, part-number engravings, and non-structural 0.5mm chamfers in your CAD system to prevent sliver elements.

Engineering Best Practices Checklist

  • Always verify Element Quality (Skewness < 0.85, Jacobian Ratio > 0.1) in Mesh Metrics before solving.
  • Never evaluate fatigue or yield failure at the boundary edge of a rigid Fixed Support.
  • Document a 3-point mesh independence table in every formal engineering simulation report.
