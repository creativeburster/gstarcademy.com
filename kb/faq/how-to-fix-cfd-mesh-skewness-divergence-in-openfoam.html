How to Fix Mesh Skewness Divergence in OpenFOAM & Fluent Simulations?

High mesh skewness (>0.85 in Fluent, maxSkewness > 4 in OpenFOAM) leads to severe interpolation errors in face normal gradients, causing pressure-velocity divergence. Fix by applying non-orthogonal correction loops in fvSolution, improving prism inflation layer transition ratios, and refining boundary layer corners.

Direct Diagnostic Solution & Protocol

Quick Resolution: High mesh skewness (>0.85 in Fluent, maxSkewness > 4 in OpenFOAM) leads to severe interpolation errors in face normal gradients, causing pressure-velocity divergence. Fix by applying non-orthogonal correction loops in fvSolution, improving prism inflation layer transition ratios, and refining boundary layer corners.

Step-by-Step Engineering Execution

Step 1: Run Mesh Diagnostic Audits

In OpenFOAM, execute checkMesh -allGeometry -allTopology. In Fluent, run Mesh Check and inspect 'Maximum Face Skewness'. Flag cells with skewness exceeding 0.85.

Step 2: Increase Non-Orthogonal Correctors in fvSolution

In system/fvSolution, increase nNonOrthogonalCorrectors from 0 to 2 or 3. This adds mathematical correction iterations to compensate for skewed face normals.

Step 3: Tune snappyHexMesh Quality Controls

In system/snappyHexMeshDict, adjust maxNonOrtho 65 and maxSkewness 4. Enable nSmoothSurfaceNormals 3 to prevent prism inflation layer collapse around acute corners.

Step 4: Lower Under-Relaxation Factors for Solver Stability

If divergence persists during initial iterations, reduce pressure under-relaxation from 0.3 to 0.2, and momentum from 0.7 to 0.5 until flow fields stabilize, then restore standard values.
