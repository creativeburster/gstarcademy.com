How to Heal Civil 3D Grading Bowtie Artifacts on Sharp Corner Daylight Slopes?

Bowtie artifacts appear when daylight slope projection rays cross each other on sharp interior corners of feature lines, creating invalid self-intersecting TIN surfaces. Heal by applying fillet corner radii to feature lines, utilizing the 'Clear Grading Bowties' tool, or converting sharp vertices into smooth arcs.

Direct Diagnostic Solution & Protocol

Quick Resolution: Bowtie artifacts appear when daylight slope projection rays cross each other on sharp interior corners of feature lines, creating invalid self-intersecting TIN surfaces. Heal by applying fillet corner radii to feature lines, utilizing the 'Clear Grading Bowties' tool, or converting sharp vertices into smooth arcs.

Step-by-Step Engineering Execution

Step 1: Understand Why Bowties Form

When projecting a 3:1 daylight slope inward from an interior 90-degree corner, offset projection vectors overlap, creating a self-intersecting butterfly loop (bowtie) that breaks surface triangulation.

Step 2: Run the 'Clear Grading Bowties' Command

Select the problematic grading object. On the contextual ribbon, click 'Clear Grading Bowties'. Click the two intersecting projection lines to allow Civil 3D to trim the overlap.

Step 3: Apply Tangent Fillets to Feature Lines

The permanent engineering fix: select the base Feature Line, launch the 'Fillet' command, and apply a 3m to 5m radius to all sharp inside corners before grading.

Step 4: Rebuild TIN Grading Surface

In the Toolspace Prospector, right-click the corridor/grading surface and select 'Rebuild Surface'. Verify that contour lines flow smoothly without cross-over loops.
