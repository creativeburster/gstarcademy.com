Southeast Asian Rail & Highway Consortium Migrates 550 Seats to GstarCAD & DWG FastView

A multinational civil engineering consortium executing high-speed rail corridors and cross-border expressways transitioned 550 CAD and site survey seats to GstarCAD 2027 and DWG FastView. Overcoming severe workstation crashes on 500MB+ terrain drawings and eliminating remote site internet licensing dependencies, the consortium achieved an 81% TCO saving while accelerating field redline approvals from 3 days to 4 hours.

1. Industrial Challenge & Recurring License Rental Bottleneck

Tasked with delivering high-speed railway viaducts, mountain tunnels, and coastal expressways across complex tropical terrain, the consortium employs 1,400 civil surveyors, alignment engineers, bridge draftsmen, and site inspectors. Alignment documentation involves continuous terrain digital elevation models (DEM), alignment clothoid spiral coordinates, and multi-disciplinary drainage networks spanning hundreds of kilometers.

Design teams previously suffered chronic productivity losses. Opening 300MB–800MB civil drawings in legacy AutoCAD Civil and vanilla AutoCAD triggered frequent out-of-memory crashes and severe viewport stutter. In remote jungle tunnel sites with zero mobile connectivity, cloud-based Named User license verification frequently expired, locking site engineers out of mission-critical construction sheets during concrete pouring schedules. The consortium's Steering Committee mandated an immediate transition to a robust, crash-resilient CAD platform with 100% offline license sovereignty.

2. 30-Day Technical Verification & Standard Compliance Protocol

A rigorous 30-day technical trial on the 142 km Rail Corridor Package 3 proved GstarCAD's superior architectural strengths:

  • Ultra-Large Drawing Handling & Memory Optimization: GstarCAD’s optimized vector database engine opened a 620MB composite terrain sheet with 28 nested Xrefs in 11.4 seconds (compared to 43.8 seconds in AutoCAD), utilizing 62% less system RAM and eliminating mid-pan crash events.
  • LandXML 1.2 & Civil Alignment Coordinate Precision: Survey teams verified that coordinate points (PNEZD) imported via LandXML and terrain contour meshes (Delaunay triangulation) matched geodetic benchmarks with sub-millimeter precision across UTM Zone 48S.
  • Integrated Field-to-Office DWG FastView Sync: Field quality engineers equipped with ruggedized Android and iPad tablets utilized DWG FastView to inspect live DWG models offline inside mountain tunnels, marking up structural rebar discrepancies and synchronizing cloud redlines immediately upon returning to base camp.
  • Automated Cut & Fill Earthwork LISP Scripts: The consortium’s custom AutoLISP volume calculation routines, utilizing bpoly boundary creation and massprop extraction, operated with 100% syntax compatibility.

Rollout was completed across 18 site camps and design offices using USB hardware dongles for remote off-grid survey stations and central GstarLM floating pools for regional engineering centers in Jakarta and Bangkok. Zero hours of project downtime were recorded during the migration.

Drawing turnaround times across all civil packages accelerated by 26%. The multi-million-dollar capital savings funded the deployment of high-density aerial LiDAR drones and automated total stations across the transit corridor.
