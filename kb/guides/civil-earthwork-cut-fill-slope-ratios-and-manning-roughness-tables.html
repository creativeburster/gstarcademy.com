Civil Earthwork Cut/Fill Slope Ratios, Bulking Factors & Manning's n Reference

Essential civil infrastructure engineering lookup tables for embankment cut/fill daylight slopes, soil swell/shrinkage factors, and open-channel Manning's n roughness coefficients.

By GstarCAD Academy Engineering Editorial Board (Suzhou Gstarsoft Co., Ltd.) Reviewed by GstarCAD Academy Editorial Team Last reviewed 2026-11-03 Editorial Process

1. Engineering Architecture & Core Methodology

Civil engineers grading highway corridors, retention ponds, and drainage channels in GstarCAD rely on standardized geotechnical Cut/Fill Slope Ratios ($H:V$), earthwork Swell/Shrinkage Factors, and Manning's Open-Channel Hydraulics to size right-of-way daylight footprints and storm culverts.

2. Step-by-Step Production Workflow & Command Modules

1. Geotechnical Cut & Fill Slope Ratios (Horizontal : Vertical)

Civil grading slopes are expressed as Horizontal Run : Vertical Rise ($H:V$):

  • 3:1 ($33.3\%$ / $18.43^\circ$) or 4:1 ($25\%$ / $14.04^\circ$): Standard vegetated highway embankment fill slopes safe for tractor mowing.
  • 2:1 ($50\%$ / $26.57^\circ$): Maximum standard compacted earth fill or soil cut slope without retaining structures.
  • 0.5:1 to 0.25:1: Competent rock cut excavations.

2. Earthwork Bank vs. Loose vs. Compacted Volume (Bulking & Shrinkage)

One Bank Cubic Meter (BCM) of in-situ clay or rock expands by 20%–50% (Swell/Bulking) when excavated into dump trucks (Loose Cubic Meters, LCM), and compresses to 0.85–0.92 Compacted Cubic Meters (CCM) under sheepsfoot rollers (except blasted rock, which retains $+25\%$ permanent swell).

3. Manning's Equation for Open-Channel & Storm Culvert Sizing

Gravity flow velocity $V$ (m/s) and discharge $Q$ ($ ext{m}^3/ ext{s}$) in channels and storm pipes are governed by Manning's formula:

$$Q = A \cdot V = \frac{1}{n} \cdot A \cdot R_h^{2/3} \cdot S^{1/2}$$

where $A$ is flow area, $R_h = A / P$ is hydraulic radius (area divided by wetted perimeter $P$), $S$ is longitudinal bed slope, and $n$ is Manning's roughness coefficient.

3. Command, Variable & Standard Reference Matrix

4. Automation Script & Reference Implementation (AutoLISP Manning's Open-Channel Discharge (Q) & Velocity Calculator)

(defun c:MANNING-CALC ( / a p n s rh v q)
  (setq a (getreal "\nEnter Flow Cross-Sectional Area A (m2): ")
        p (getreal "\nEnter Wetted Perimeter P (m): ")
        n (getreal "\nEnter Manning's Roughness n (e.g. 0.013 Concrete, 0.010 PVC): ")
        s (getreal "\nEnter Channel Longitudinal Slope S (m/m, e.g. 0.005): "))
  (setq rh (/ a p)
        v  (* (/ 1.0 n) (expt rh (/ 2.0 3.0)) (sqrt s))
        q  (* a v))
  (princ (strcat "\n[Manning Hydraulics] Rh = " (rtos rh 2 3) " m | Velocity V = "
                 (rtos v 2 2) " m/s | Discharge Q = " (rtos q 2 3) " m3/s"))
  (princ)
)

5. Production QA Verification & Competency Checklist

  • Multiply raw TIN surface Fill volumes by 1.10 to 1.15 (shrinkage factor) when balancing Cut vs. Fill earthwork.
  • Compute horizontal daylight offset distance in CAD as Offset = Delta_Elevation * Slope_Ratio (e.g., 3.0 m drop at 3:1 = 9.0 m offset).
  • Use GstarCAD's AREA command on closed channel cross-section polylines to extract exact Flow Area (A) and Perimeter (P) for Manning's equation.

1. DWG Header & Registry Persistence Scope

Variables and object properties fall into three persistence scopes: Drawing-Saved (DXF Header) variables that travel with the .dwg file to subcontractors, Registry-Saved (Profile .arg) settings that persist per Windows workstation user, and Session-Volatile states that reset on every launch unless locked inside acaddoc.lsp. Always verify persistence scope via SYSVDLG before issuing sheets to an ISO 19650 Common Data Environment (CDE).

2. Multi-Core Regeneration & Memory Hygiene

When processing large civil corridors, dense plant P&IDs, or 50-sheet architectural Xref trees, unpurged Registered Application IDs (REGAPP), orphaned DGN stroke dictionaries, and redundant annotation scales degrade spatial index (INDEXCTL = 3) lookup speed.
