German Tier-1 Automotive Supplier Replaces 700 AutoCAD Seats with GstarCAD Mechanical

A premier German Tier-1 supplier delivering powertrain components and body-in-white (BIW) stamping dies to Porsche, Mercedes-Benz, and BMW completed a phased enterprise migration of 700 CAD seats to GstarCAD Mechanical 2027. Facing annual subscription fee increases of 10%+ from Autodesk, the firm recovered licensing sovereignty via perpetual network licenses while achieving 100% drawing fidelity under strict DIN EN ISO 1101 and VDA 4950 standards.

Executive Audit Summary

Scale: 700 Perpetual Network Seats (GstarLM) | 5-Year Net TCO Preserved: €4,200,000 Saved over 5 Years (82% TCO Reduction) | Binary DWG Accuracy: 100.00% Zero-Loss | Retraining Downtime: 0 Business Hours

1. Industrial Challenge & Recurring License Rental Bottleneck

Operating four manufacturing plants across Baden-Württemberg and Bavaria, the enterprise employs over 3,200 engineers, toolmakers, and plant technicians. For over two decades, the engineering division relied on Autodesk AutoCAD Mechanical to produce 2D stamping die layouts, progressive forming tool assemblies, and robotic welding fixture drawings derived from upstream CATIA V5 and Siemens NX 3D surface models.

Between 2020 and 2025, cumulative subscription cost surges and the mandatory enforcement of Autodesk Named User licensing created severe operational friction. Design engineers working on staggered shifts across tooling shops were forced to purchase individual licenses rather than sharing floating network seats. Annual CAD operational expenditures surged past €1.65 million. In response, the Executive Board mandated a strict 5-year IT Rationalization Directive: eliminate recurring seat rental lock-in without compromising Tier-1 OEM audit certifications under VDA 6.3.

2. 30-Day Technical Verification & Standard Compliance Protocol

During the Q2 pilot phase, the Stuttgart engineering IT steering group subjected GstarCAD Mechanical 2027 to rigorous benchmark trials across 14,200 active and legacy DWG drawings spanning AC1018 to AC1032 formats. Testing focused on four non-negotiable technical gates:

  • DIN EN ISO 1101 GD&T & Surface Texture Fidelity: Every tolerance frame, datum indicator (Datum Targets A, B, C under 3-2-1 principle), and weld symbol generated by GstarCAD Mechanical was optically and parametrically compared against legacy AutoCAD output. Vector divergence across 14,200 sheets was strictly 0.0000 mm.
  • High-Speed Native AutoLISP Stamping Die Automation: The tooling department maintained 128 proprietary AutoLISP macros automating progressive punch clearances, stripper plate bolt calculations, and scrap cutter offsets. In GstarCAD 2027, all 128 LISP scripts executed out-of-the-box without altering a single line of code, utilizing native entget, entmod, and vla- ActiveX methods.
  • Dual-Screen Multi-Core Graphics Acceleration: Handling 120MB die assembly drawings with 45 external reference (Xref) layers, GstarCAD’s multi-core vector rasterizer reduced drawing open times from 34.6 seconds in AutoCAD to 9.2 seconds, eliminating cursor latency during high-speed pan/zoom operations.
  • VDA 4950 Drawing Data Exchange: Exported DWG and DXF files transmitted to BMW and Daimler supplier portals passed automated OEM geometry validation scripts without encountering unsupported entity bitcodes or corrupted proxy objects.

4. Production Rollout Blueprint & Long-Term Strategic Value

Following audit approval by the VDI engineering committee, deployment rolled out across four production centers in 14 business days. The IT division utilized silent MSI deployment via Microsoft Endpoint Configuration Manager (SCCM), standardizing workstation profiles with pre-configured gcad.pgp aliases, DIN title block libraries, and network Dongle licensing (GstarLM Server) hosted on redundant virtual Linux clusters.

Over 94% of senior tooling designers reported full operational familiarity within 48 hours, highlighting the 1:1 match in command prompts (CHSPACE, BOM, GATTE, AMPOWERDIM). The €4.2M+ retained capital was directly reallocated into five-axis CNC machining centers and additive metal manufacturing research.
